The steel the required section is made of, at `ρ = 7850 kg/m³`, against
the arm line mass the model assumed for it.

| hub arms (12) | (iii) minimum **area** over the grid | 4.417 | 15.000 | **0.294** | yes |

**For the rows marked NO, the section needed to pass outweighs the mass assumed
for it, so the mass basis and the section are inconsistent.** The model holds the
deck's `μ` fixed and back-computes an equivalent density, so a heavier section
carries no more self-weight in the solve — which is exactly why the search
converges and exactly why the answer cannot be taken at face value. What that
section would really weigh implies more self-weight, hence a larger required
section, and so on.

**This is not iterated here.** Closing that loop needs a mass budget, which is
increment 2.

**How big the inconsistency is, in the only units that matter here.** The
arm-weight term above is the required section's own steel acting on its own
span; the published static root moment already includes the body's remainder
mass. So the comparison says whether the arm alone, correctly weighed, would
outweigh everything the static case currently carries:

```
  i_min_wall_at_D_2.5          arm weight alone      2.590481e+08 N.m
                               the WHOLE published static root 1.916016e+08 N.m
                               ratio                 1.352
  ii_min_diameter_at_t_180mm   arm weight alone      1.643348e+08 N.m
                               the WHOLE published static root 1.916016e+08 N.m
                               ratio                 0.858
```

**At the tightest answer the arm's own steel alone would exceed the entire
current static root moment by 35%** — before the
remainder mass, before any wave load, and before the larger section that
extra weight would itself require. That is the size of the circularity
increment 2 has to break, and it is why this increment stops here rather
than iterating.

## Findings

**(i) Every search converges, so a passing section exists in the declared grid** —
which F6 could not say. The platform arms need the most steel by every measure.

**(ii) At the recorded diameter the platform arm needs `t = 410 mm`**,
against the recorded `180 mm` — `D/t = 6.10`, a
very thick-walled tube, and its steel weighs
`2.254×` the mass assumed for it. Holding `D` is the most
expensive of the three constraints.

**(iii) The minimum-area answers are the only mass-consistent ones**, and both
sit at `D = 6.0 m` — **the upper bound of the declared grid**. That is
a boundary answer and should be read as one: the true minimum-area tube may lie
outside `D ≤ 6.0 m`, and the search cannot say, because FG3 fixed the range.
Their `D/t` are `109.09` and `200.00` — slender, where
§ 3.2.2(b)'s local-buckling reduction is live rather than inert as it is on F6's
stand-in.

**(iv) THE TWO CONSTRAINED PLATFORM ANSWERS LAND ON THE SAME SECTION MODULUS,
and that is the table's explanation rather than a coincidence.**

```
  (i)  D = 2.5 m, t = 410 mm -> W = 1.221159 m^3, A = 2.6920 m^2
  (ii) D = 3.2 m, t = 180 mm -> W = 1.221162 m^3, A = 1.7078 m^2
  the same W to six figures, from areas differing by 1.576x
```

**Bending governs the platform arms, so the binding quantity is `W`, not `A`.**
Two searches constrained in different variables converge on the same minimum `W`
and pay 1.576× different amounts of steel for it. That is
why (iii) finds so much less area at a larger diameter: `W` grows with `D²t`
while `A` grows with `Dt`, so diameter buys section modulus far more cheaply
than wall thickness does. **If the real arm is a truss, this is the number that
transfers** — a truss buys `W` with depth in exactly the same way.

The hub arms are governed by § 3.3.1 in **tension** at every answer, so there it
is `A` that binds and the three answers do not share a `W`.

**(v) Neither modelling lever moves the answer**, as in F6: compare the `U(K=1)` and
`U(C_m=1)` columns with `max U`. Section 3.3.2 takes the larger of its amplified and
simple forms and the simple form carries neither `F_a` nor `C_m`.

**(vi) The premise the search rests on is verified, not assumed.** Section choice
does not move the member forces: rebuilt at the minimum-area answers and compared
over all 48 member-stations and six components, the worst relative change is
`1.608328e-15` — round-off. And `U ≤ 1.0` holds on the rebuilt forces too:
`0.581954` on the platform group, `0.821927` on the hubs, on the static basis.
`scripts/measure/f6a_premise_check.py` is the command, and it reports the two
earlier normalisations that divided noise by noise before that figure was
trustworthy.

**What this does not answer:** the real truss, the mass budget, any heading but 0°,
and whether `F_y = 355 MPa` is the grade. The label at the top is the whole of what
these numbers are.
