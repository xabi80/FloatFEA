## Mass consistency — the key finding

The steel the required section is made of, at `ρ = 7850 kg/m³`, against
the arm line mass the model assumed for it.

| group | search | ρ·A_req [t/m] | assumed μ [t/m] | ratio | consistent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| platform arms (4) | (i) `D = 2.5 m` fixed, minimum `t` | 21.132 | 9.375 | **2.254** | NO — the section outweighs its own assumed mass |
| platform arms (4) | (ii) `t = 180 mm` fixed, minimum `D` | 13.406 | 9.375 | **1.430** | NO — the section outweighs its own assumed mass |
| platform arms (4) | (iii) minimum **area** over the grid | 8.064 | 9.375 | **0.860** | yes |
| hub arms (12) | (i) `D = 2.5 m` fixed, minimum `t` | 8.148 | 15.000 | **0.543** | yes |
| hub arms (12) | (ii) `t = 180 mm` fixed, minimum `D` | 9.411 | 15.000 | **0.627** | yes |
| hub arms (12) | (iii) minimum **area** over the grid | 4.417 | 15.000 | **0.294** | yes |

**For the rows marked NO, the section needed to pass outweighs the mass assumed
for it, so the mass basis and the section are inconsistent.** The model holds the
deck's `μ` fixed and back-computes an equivalent density, so a heavier section
carries no more self-weight in the solve — which is exactly why the search
converges and exactly why the answer cannot be taken at face value. What that
section would really weigh implies more self-weight, hence a larger required
section, and so on.

**This is not iterated here.** Closing that loop needs a mass budget, which is
increment 2.

**How big the inconsistency is, and the arm-weight term alone UNDERSTATES it.**
The published static root moment carries two independent loads: the arm's own
uniform weight, and the body's remainder mass at its own node. **Only the first
moves with the section.** So hold the remainder at its published value and scale
the arm:

```
  g L^2 / 2                    = 1.225831e+04 N.m per (kg/m)
  published static root |My|   = 1.916016e+08 N.m
  of which the remainder's share = 7.667992e+07 N.m  (40.0%), constant in the section

search                          mu implied       arm term    + remainder  /published
i_min_wall_at_D_2.5              21132.441   2.590481e+08   3.357280e+08       1.752
ii_min_diameter_at_t_180mm       13405.993   1.643348e+08   2.410148e+08       1.258
iii_min_area                      8063.695   9.884729e+07   1.755272e+08       0.916
```

**2 of the three platform answers imply a static root moment ABOVE the present total**, the worst by `75%` —
before any wave load, and before the larger section that extra weight would
itself require. **An earlier version of this document compared the arm-weight
term alone against the published total and reported a smaller figure, which
understated it, and showed answer (ii) as under the total when it is over.**
That is the size of the circularity increment 2 has to break, and it is why
this increment stops here rather than iterating.

**And the two-point fit that looks like the obvious way to measure this is
the wrong instrument.** Fitting `M_root = a·μ + b` from two
`measurement_fraction` builds gives a **negative** `a`: raising `f` takes mass
off the remainder node and spreads it along the arm, and the remainder's lever
is larger per kilogram, so the f-line's slope is not the arm-weight
coefficient. The sign is what says so.

## Findings

**(i) Every search converges, so a passing section exists in the declared grid** —
which F6 could not say. The platform arms need the most steel by every measure.

**(ii) At the recorded diameter the platform arm needs `t = 410 mm`**,
against the recorded `180 mm` — `D/t = 6.10`, a
very thick-walled tube, and its steel weighs
`2.254×` the mass assumed for it. Holding `D` is the most
expensive of the three constraints.

**(iii) The minimum-area answers are the only mass-consistent ones**, and both
sit at `D = 6.0 m` — **the upper bound of the declared grid**. That is
a boundary answer and should be read as one: the true minimum-area tube may lie
outside `D ≤ 6.0 m`, and the search cannot say, because FG3 fixed the range.

**Which direction that matters in, and how fast — because the bound is not a
detail here.** The least area a passing section needs falls as the bound rises,
by 66.6% across the range, so RAISING the bound would only make this answer lighter and more
consistent. Lowering it does the opposite, and the mass-consistency verdict does
not survive far:

| `D` [m] | min passing `A` [m²] | `ρ·A / μ` | mass-consistent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | 1.8811 | **1.575** | NO |
| 4.0 | 1.3443 | **1.126** | NO |
| 4.9 | 1.2114 | **1.014** | NO |
| 5.0 | 1.1604 | **0.972** | yes |
| 6.0 | 1.0272 | **0.860** | yes |

Nothing in the grid passes below `D = 2.4 m` for this group. The ratio is above 1.0 up to and including `D = 4.9 m` and at or below it from `D = 5.0 m`, so **had FG3
written `D ≤ 4.9 m` this finding would have read the other way** — every answer inconsistent, and no
mass-consistent section anywhere in the declared range. The conclusion is a
function of the bound and the bound was given, not derived.

The sequence is **not monotone**: 9 of 36 steps in `D` cost MORE area than the diameter below them, the
largest by 2.38%, because the 5 mm wall step quantises the answer and a
0.1 m step in `D` can land on a thicker admissible wall. The trend falls; the
grid does not, and an earlier version of this paragraph said it did. What a bound
selects is the best section at or below it, and that does fall — which is the
claim the paragraph needs and the one it now makes.
Their `D/t` are `109.09` and `200.00` — slender, where
§ 3.2.2(b)'s local-buckling reduction is live rather than inert as it is on F6's
stand-in.

**(iv) BENDING GOVERNS THE PLATFORM ARMS, SO THE BINDING QUANTITY IS `W` AND NOT
`A` — and the number that transfers is the continuum one, not the grid's.**

```
bending is 99.98595% of U at the governing station (platform:hub2_arm ROOT, T = 12.5 s),
so the requirement is a section modulus:
  W_req = M_res / F_b = 3.236906e+08 / 2.662500e+08 = 1.215739 m^3    (F_b branch: compact)

inverting max U = 1 in each constrained search, instead of sampling the grid:
  D = 2.5 m fixed : t* = 406.4985 mm  -> W* = 1.21591028
  t = 180 mm fixed: D* = 3.193772 m  -> W* = 1.21600748
  the two EXACT requirements differ by 7.994e-05; the two GRID answers by 2.565e-06
  one grid step in W at D = 2.5 m is 0.007515 m^3, so that agreement is 4.2e-04 of ONE step
```

**The mechanism is a measurement; the six-figure agreement was not.** Both
constrained answers sit in the same compact branch, so `F_b` is identically
`0.75 F_y` and the required `W` is the same quantity in both searches — that part
is real, and it is why two searches constrained in different variables pay
`1.576×` different amounts of steel for it. **But the
mechanism is only good to the spread above, and an earlier version of this
document published the grid's much closer agreement as though it were the
structure.** A reader transferring six figures to a truss would have been
over-trusting the measurement by orders of magnitude.

**Transfer `W_req ≈ 1.2157 m³`, and only with its
allowable**: it is tied to `F_b = 266.25 MPa`, the
compact branch. Answer (iii) does **not** share it — at
`D/t = 109.09` § 3.2.3 reduces `F_b`, so its `W` is `1.5128` against the compact requirement above.

`W` grows with `D²t` and `A` with `Dt`, so diameter buys section modulus far more
cheaply than wall thickness — which is why (iii) finds so much less area at a
larger diameter, and is the part that carries over to a truss, which buys `W`
with depth the same way.

The hub arms are governed by § 3.3.1 in **tension** at every answer, so there it
is `A` that binds and the three answers share no `W` — the control on the
sentence above rather than a second example of it.

**(v) Neither modelling lever moves the answer**, as in F6: compare the `U(K=1)` and
`U(C_m=1)` columns with `max U`. Section 3.3.2 takes the larger of its amplified and
simple forms and the simple form carries neither `F_a` nor `C_m`.

**(vi) The premise the search rests on is verified, and it is NARROWER than it was
first stated.** The claim was that section choice does not move the member forces.
On the static case that is true to round-off; on the dynamic case **the forces do
move**, and what does not move is `max U` — the quantity the search's answer
depends on — after re-selecting which instant governs at the sized section.

**The mechanism, because the first explanation was wrong.** It said the dynamic
increment comes from FloatSim's multipliers and each body's d'Alembert field, and
the field reads `μ`, which is held — so no section could reach it. But the field is
an acceleration, and what turns it into a load is `M_e @ a_e` with the consistent
element mass matrix, whose rotary terms are `ρ_eq·I = μ·r²`: **`r` moves with the
section even though `μ` does not.** `scripts/measure/f6a_premise_check.py --dynamic`
is the command, and it also reports the two earlier normalisations that divided
noise by noise before the static figure was trustworthy.

**What this does not answer:** the real truss, the mass budget, any heading but 0°,
and whether `F_y = 355 MPa` is the grade. The label at the top is the whole of what
these numbers are.
