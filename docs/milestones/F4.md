### DQ4. The `M·a` test

EJ5 names DQ4 as the `M·a` test. **Proposed reading, for the lock to confirm or
replace:** a load case built by prescribing a known rigid-body acceleration `a` on a
body, with no hydrodynamic and no external load, must produce a nodal force set whose
resultant is `M·a` and whose moment about the body reference is `J·alpha`, per body
and about a common point. It is the mapping half of V4.2 — it tests that the FE mass
matrix distributes an acceleration correctly before any constraint is involved, so a
failure here is read before G4.2's stress figure is read at all.

**Open:** whether DQ4's subject is the 5 structural bodies of DW0/DX0 or all 17.

### DQ5. V4.2 / G4.2, re-specified from an independent body force

`docs/verification/README.md:139-142` states V4.2 as "a load case built from pure
rigid-body acceleration with no external load produces near-zero internal stress".
EJ5 directs that it be re-specified **from an independent body force**. **Proposed
reading:** the acceleration is not taken from the solve and not taken from the same
mass matrix the FE assembles. The case is driven by a body force computed
independently — a prescribed uniform field times a mass distribution read from the
deck — so that a mass matrix which is wrong in the same way on both sides cannot
cancel. The gate remains near-zero internal stress.

**Open:** what the independent source is. Candidates: the deck's own per-body `mass`
and `J_G` (the DZ5 values), or an analytic mass distribution over the F3 frame. The
first is independent of the FE assembly but not of the deck; the second is
independent of both and matches nothing physical.

### DQ6. The window and the case set

EJ4 ran one case at `T = 14 s` full scale. **Open:** the steady-state window (start
time and length), and whether F4 maps all six design-wave cases or one while the
mapping is being built. See § 6.

One measurement bears on it and it is **not** restated here: the window used for
EJ4(b)'s residual is under a period, chosen for comparability with verdict 87's figure
and inadequate as a steady-state window. The figure and its arithmetic are in
`docs/reports/F3/step-3.md` revision 4 § 3, which is regenerated by rule. Whatever the
lock chooses should be a whole number of periods after the ramp.

### DQ7. Buoys: loads or bodies

F3 scoped the model to five structural bodies with the buoys as loads (DW0, DX0).
**Open:** whether F4's mapping keeps that, which makes each buoy's gimbal reaction a
point load at `buoy_joint_nodes`, or whether any buoy becomes FE mass.

### DQ8. What the per-body equilibrium residual is normalised by

G4.1 says "as a fraction of total applied load magnitude" (`PLAN.md:309`). With the
static part reconstructed in FloatFEA, the denominator is no longer small: weight
dominates. **Open:** whether the fraction is against the total including the
reconstructed static part, or against the dynamic part alone, and the consequence
either way is a different tolerance.

## 1. SCOPE, AND THE ONE THING F3 GOT WRONG ABOUT IT

F4 maps FloatSim's exported loads onto the F3 frame's nodes by physical origin,
reconstructs the static part, solves with inertia relief per body, and extracts
member forces.

**The static part is FloatFEA's to build, and this is the decision F3's closure
artifact misread once.** `docs/load-interchange-v1.md:618-619` locks `gravity` and
`hydrostatic` **out** of the schema: gravity "computed in FloatFEA from the FE mass
distribution", hydrostatic "recomputed in FloatFEA from hull geometry, on the MEAN
wetted surface", because `C` is a restoring derivative and carries no pressure field.
`:670` records that adding either channel "would read as improvements to someone who
had not read this table". `PLAN.md:326-332` prescribes the reconstruction and makes
**G4.6** the gate that proves it against FloatSim's own `C`.

`docs/closure/F3.md` § 6a (i) measures the consequence on this deck: the equilibrium
reaction FloatSim carries is identically `0`, `xi = 0` is an exact equilibrium of a
model with no gravity term, and five structural bodies totalling `568.98 N` of weight
appear nowhere in its balance. **So the static part is not recoverable from anything
exported, and it was never meant to be.**

## 2. STEP 1 — LOAD MAPPING

### 2.1 What it builds

1. **The reader and the mapper.** Each v1 record's load reaches the FE nodes of the
   body its label names, by physical origin, preserving total force and moment about
   a common point (G4.4). Distributed loads arrive strip-resolved; a record with no
   strip data is refused, not distributed by assumption.
2. **The joint reactions.** `res.lam` per joint — three forces and the locked-axis
   moment — applied at `buoy_joint_nodes` and at the four hub-to-platform nodes.
3. **Gravity**, from the FE mass distribution (`PLAN.md:303`), per body.
4. **Buoyancy**, from the hull geometry on the **mean** wetted surface
   (`PLAN.md:337-340`). Not the instantaneous surface: that introduces a
   nonlinearity FloatSim never had, and it surfaces as a G4.1 residual with no
   obvious cause.
5. **The additive HSP writer** (`scripts/`, outside the gate, started under EJ4). It
   carries `res.lam`, the per-body external force, and `mu[N,6]` — already a required
   v1 channel at `docs/load-interchange-v1.md:87`. **It does not carry an equilibrium
   reaction**, because § 6a (i) measures that reaction as zero.

### 2.2 The EB6 label-provenance gate

A buoy-label permutation in the deck export passes the shipped suite — the count is
in `docs/closure/F3.md` § 6, which is where it was measured, and is deliberately not
restated here — and `buoy_joint_nodes` is how every gimbal reaction is applied. So the
gate's expected side is read **read-only from FloatSim's own platform definition in
HSP-stable**, not from the export:

```
# expected: HSP-stable/studies/platform-12buoy/platform_common.py:51-58
#           buoy_centers() -- (12, 2) horizontal centres, buoy k = 3c+b, built from
#           CLUSTER_ARM_RADIUS (:33), CLUSTER_ANGLES_DEG (:34), BUOY_ANGLES_DEG (:35)
#           and BUOY_RADIUS (:36)
# second expected side: the cluster angle, CLUSTER_ANGLES_DEG (:34), checked
#           independently of the centres so that a consistent rotation of all twelve
#           is caught by one side even where the other cannot see it
```

Each buoy label's node coordinates must equal that buoy's position from this source.
**The permuted export must redden it** — that is the gate's counter-case, and it is
constructed, not asserted.

## 3. STEP 2 — SOLVE: INERTIA RELIEF PER BODY

Inertia relief via Lagrange multipliers on six rigid-body constraints per body, which
has the property `PLAN.md:305-307` names: the multipliers **are** the residual
reactions and double as a diagnostic.

* **G4.1 is per-body equilibrium**, reported per case and per body, never aggregated.
  An outlier hidden in a mean is the specific thing this diagnostic exists to catch
  (CLAUDE.md, *Never average a per-case diagnostic*).
* **DQ4** — the `M·a` test, as § 0 settles it.
* **DQ5** — V4.2 / G4.2 re-specified from an independent body force, as § 0 settles
  it.
* **G4.3** — a pure hydrostatic case reproduces the analytically known buoyancy
  distribution.
* **G4.6** — the recomputed buoyancy, linearised about `xi = 0`, reproduces
  FloatSim's `C` per body, 6x6. This is the gate that confirms the reconstruction
  matches the model that generated the motions; disagreement means the hull geometry
  or the waterplane differs between the two models.
* **G4.5** — residual convergence *rate* under timestep refinement, at `dt` and
  `dt/2`. The rate, not the magnitude: a re-introduced one-step lag reverts the rate
  to first order while the absolute residual can still look acceptable.

**The residual form is the discrete one, and that is a decision already taken.** The
continuous identity does not close to round-off: `newmark.py:48` documents
`mu_{n+1-alpha_f} ~= mu_n` as an O(h) lag, and `docs/load-interchange-v1.md`
§ 4.1-4.2 chose the discrete form so that G4.1 means what it says.
`scripts/report_joint_reactions.py::discrete_residual` forms it today.

## 8. WHAT F4 DOES NOT DO

* It does not add a `gravity` or `hydrostatic` channel to the interchange schema.
  Both are § 7 decisions and `:670` says what adding them would look like.
* It does not modify FloatSim's solve path. The HSP writer is additive, in `scripts/`,
  and inert when output is disabled.
* It does not invent a load distribution. A record with no strip resolution is
  refused.
* It does not open new apparatus — no new guards, scanners, meta-tests or detectors
  (DR1, no new apparatus through F6).
* It does not solve the shell sub-models. Those are exported to CalculiX.
