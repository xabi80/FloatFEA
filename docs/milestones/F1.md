## 1. What F1 is, after the audit

PLAN.md §4 opens F1 with the G1.0 output audit, on the expectation that the
audit might shrink the HSP work to a converter living in FloatFEA. It did the
opposite. The audit is substantively complete and its finding is that
**FloatSim writes nothing usable today, and most of what the schema assumed
would be available does not exist in any form.**

That is not a setback. It is the cheapest hour in the project working exactly
as PLAN.md §4 predicted it would, and it arrived before any code was written
against a wrong assumption.

The consequence is that F1 is a build rather than a conversion, and that the
schema in `docs/load-interchange-v1.md` needs revision before it is locked —
four of its channel groups describe quantities the simulator does not compute.

## 2. Audit findings that shape this milestone

Recorded here in summary. The G1.0 finding document carries the full evidence.

**Nothing is written to disk.** `floatsim/io/results.py` is still the
Milestone-0 stub and says so in its own docstring. The deck's
`Output(file=..., channels=[...])` field has no consumer anywhere in the
codebase. The only results persisted are heave-only CSVs and an npz of point
kinematics under `studies/platform-12buoy/fin_study/`. Zero load channels
exist on disk.

**Most of what is needed exists in memory.** `IntegrationResult` carries
`t, xi, xi_dot, xi_ddot, lam`. The `lam` history is the joint constraint
force — 16 joints × 4 rows = 64 multipliers, documented dt-free and physical
at `floatsim/solver/newmark.py:132-137`. For the dry structure this is the
entire external load path, exact, already computed, and discarded at write
time.

**Four schema channel groups do not exist and cannot be recovered.**

| Schema group | Reality |
|---|---|
| `quaternion[N,4]` | FloatSim is small-angle linear Euler throughout; there is no finite-rotation state |
| `gravity`, `hydrostatic` | Both live only inside the linearised restoring matrix `C`; at ξ=0 they cancel and there is no net force to export |
| `froude_krylov`, `diffraction` | BEM gives one combined `F_exc(ω)` per body; not separable |
| `radiation` | Per-body 6-vector; `μ(t)` is a loop local in `newmark.py` and is never stored |

**Two load sources are already distribution-resolved.** The spar carries 10
Morison segments (`distributed_cylinder_drag(..., n_segments=10)`), each
evaluated at its own midpoint and summed at `morison.py:848`. The heave plate
is integrated over a polar patch quadrature, and `df_n` at `morison.py:583`
is a genuine per-patch pressure field across the disc — exactly what a
plate-bending model needs, at full resolution, one line before it is summed
away. The edge-on term is lumped at the disc centre and is *not*
patch-resolved; the record must say so.

**The small-angle basis is at risk in the cases that will govern.**
`docs/phase2-followups.md:1364` (INBAND-ROTATIONAL-RESONANCE) records that
the articulated buoy-pitch mode at T_rot = 3.257 s has Q ≈ 134 and that its
drag-free rotation reaches 0.1 rad at ~4.5 mm wave amplitude, against 5.4 m
off resonance. The platform fan runs at 20–60 mm amplitude and explicitly
sweeps the mode. Drag was made mandatory in M11 to control this; how much it
controls it is not recorded anywhere, because every stored output is
heave-only. No rotation channel is saved in any file in the repository.

## 3. Decisions taken

**Panel pressures** for the BEM-sourced loads. Capytaine post-processing
produces per-panel complex pressure fields for the diffraction problem and
each radiation DOF. This is the only route that yields genuinely distributed
loads for excitation and radiation without a modelling assumption, and it
respects the CLAUDE.md prohibition on inventing a distribution.

**Full scale, 50:1.** FloatSim runs at full scale with a full-scale deck and
a full-scale BEM re-run, rather than model results being scaled at the reader
boundary. Froude scaling is dimensionally inhomogeneous — a different
exponent per channel — so a wrong exponent is a silent factor-of-50 rather
than an obviously broken number, and CLAUDE.md's "conversion only at the I/O
boundary" rule was not written with that in mind. Running at full scale means
no conversion anywhere, no `scale` block in the schema, and no per-channel
exponent table. It also allows drag coefficients to be set at full-scale
Reynolds rather than inherited from model-scale Re, which is the one quantity
Froude scaling corrupts (KC is preserved; Re goes up 354×). The model-scale
case is retained as a cross-check.

**Rotation vector (axis-angle)**, carried verbatim as FloatSim computes it. No
synthesised quaternions: FloatSim has no finite-rotation state, and a
quaternion channel would advertise a validity the source does not have.

**Reconstructed with Rodrigues, never with the linearised map** — now mandated
by `docs/conventions.md` § Rotations. The rotation vector is an *exact*
parameterisation; only FloatSim's internal *use* of it is linearised. Applying
`I + [θ×]` inside FloatFEA produces a non-orthogonal matrix that stretches
every transformed vector by `√(1+θ²)` — 1.5% at 10°, 4.3% at 17° — silently
magnifying every transformed load. Rodrigues is orthogonal by construction and
costs nothing. This supersedes the earlier reasoning in this document that
FloatFEA should reproduce FloatSim's linearisation for consistency: that would
import an error rather than avoid one, and G4.1's residual is better served by
an exact transform.

What Rodrigues cannot do is recover information the linearisation never
carried. It yields an exact rotation from whatever θ FloatSim reports; whether
that θ is itself right at the observed amplitudes is a separate, upstream
question — see Q2 in §10.

**Gravity is computed in FloatFEA** from the FE mass distribution, not
imported. It is the one load source FloatFEA knows better than FloatSim,
which carries a lumped placeholder. The schema states this explicitly so that
nobody goes looking for a channel that was deliberately omitted.

**Tag `928ae47`** (current tip of main) as the HSP reference, once the venv is
repaired. `floatsim/`, `tests/` and `pyproject.toml` are byte-identical
between `e69f53c` — the last commit touching the package — and HEAD; all 28
intervening commits touched only `studies/` and `docs/`. The 12-buoy platform
model lives entirely under `studies/`, so tagging earlier would exclude the
thing being analysed.

## 4. What gets built

**In HSP — additive, none of it on the solve path.**

**Build order: solve state, then strip/patch, then panel pressures.** The
original ordering put strip/patch first on the expectation that patch-resolving
the plate tangential term would also settle the drift investigation. **That
rationale is withdrawn** — the drift is driven by the plate *normal* term, which
is already patch-resolved, so the tangential work will not resolve it (see
`docs/findings/mean-drift-and-screening.md`). Re-sequenced on export grounds
alone, solve state wins on three counts: it is the smallest of the three; it is
the only one producing a file the reader can be developed against immediately;
and it carries the alpha-state alignment decision below, which is a **G4.1
precondition** and not something to discover in F4.

> **The alpha-state alignment problem — new, and not covered by §6.**
> §6 aligns each exported force to the index of the state it was evaluated from,
> which fixes the `state_force` / `external_force` split. **It does not fix
> this.** Generalized-alpha forms its right-hand side at *alpha-weighted* states:
> the balance is `(1−α_f)·F_{n+1} + α_f·F_n` (`newmark.py:415-422`), with
> `α_f = ρ_∞/(1+ρ_∞) = 0.4737` at the default `ρ_∞ = 0.9`. So the force the
> integrator actually applied over a step is a **blend of two steps' forces
> evaluated at a blended state**, and no single-index export represents it
> exactly. The residual this leaves is of order `ω·α_f·Δt` — the same few-percent
> band as the lag §6 removed, and it lands in the same place: G4.1.
>
> The decision — export the alpha-weighted combination, export both endpoints and
> let the reader blend, or declare the residual — must be made **with the solve
> state module**, because it determines what that module writes. It is recorded
> here so it is made deliberately rather than defaulted into.

Also: `mu`'s closed-form validation (§5) is ready to run the day the channel
exists. Building solve state first means the newest and most error-prone piece in
the schema is verified immediately, rather than waiting behind two larger
modules.

1. **Panel-pressure export.** Capytaine post-processing producing per-panel
   complex pressure fields for diffraction and each radiation DOF, on the
   existing hull mesh. New module, new CLI entry point.
2. **Strip and patch load export.** Per-Morison-element and per-plate-patch
   loads captured before summation. This is the replay hook, and it must be
   inert when disabled — G1.5 proves it.
3. **Solve-state export.** What `IntegrationResult` already carries, plus the
   joint definitions needed to interpret `lam` (the multipliers are rows
   against the constraint Jacobian, not a force/moment 6-vector, so the
   geometry must travel with them).

**In FloatFEA.**

4. Reader and validator for `.flr` v1.
5. Schema revision per §2: remove the four non-existent channel groups, add
   the panel-pressure group, the rotation validity bound, and the force
   timestamp convention of §6.
6. The screening metric list, fixed here because it constrains the schema
   (PLAN.md §5).

## 5. Gates

**G1.0** — written finding. Substantively complete; needs writing up. Blocks
the schema lock.

**G1.1** — round-trip, bit-exact, every channel. Unchanged.

**G1.2** — validator rejections, each with a specific message. Extended from
the list in `docs/load-interchange-v1.md` §4 with: rotation exceeding the
declared validity bound, a missing or ambiguous force timestamp declaration,
and failure of the G1.6 integration check.

**G1.3** — provenance. Unchanged.

**G1.4** — reinterpreted. With regular waves only, no RNG anywhere in the
solve path, and irregular seas still Phase 2, replay determinism is close to
free and the storage pressure that motivated two-pass replay does not exist
yet. The determinism test is still run (§9) but is no longer a gate. What
survives of G1.4 is its numerical half, promoted to G1.6.

**G1.5** — additive-only, proven by bit-identical HSP regression results on
the export branch and at the tag. Currently unfalsifiable: see §9.

**G1.6 — reconstruction integrity.** Adopted into PLAN.md §6 F1. Per-panel pressures and
per-strip loads, integrated over each hull, reproduce the body-level force
FloatSim actually applied — excitation, radiation, drag — **reported per body
and per source**, never aggregated. This is V4.5 generalised from strips to
panels, and it is the gate that stops the structure being loaded with a
pressure field that looks reasonable but is not what the simulator computed.

Three requirements on how it reports:

- **Per body, per source.** Harmonic content from the nonlinear drag and the
  joint constraints does not distribute evenly — it concentrates near the free
  surface and on the heave plate. A global residual lets a bad plate average
  away against clean spar panels, which is the failure mode the per-case rule
  in CLAUDE.md already exists to prevent.
- **Spectral content of the residual.** If the residual sits at 2ω and 3ω,
  that says directly that fundamental-only reconstruction is insufficient and
  indicates what order of convolution would be needed.
- **Diagnostic, not only pass/fail.** The reconstruction risk is real: linear
  hydro responds at whatever frequencies the motion contains, and the motion
  is periodic but not sinusoidal because drag and the joint constraints are
  nonlinear. Reconstructing at the fundamental is cheap and probably
  sufficient; the general answer is a per-panel retardation convolution and is
  expensive. Start at the fundamental and let G1.6 measure the residual, so
  the escalation is triggered by a measurement rather than a judgement.

## 6. The force timestamp convention

`state_force` is evaluated at `(t[n], xi_n, xi_dot_n)` and applied to the
step-(n+1) right-hand side (`newmark.py:409`), while `external_force` is
evaluated at `t_{n+1}` (`newmark.py:401`). Two different timestamps inside
the same RHS.

If the exporter writes both at index n+1 and FloatFEA pairs them with
kinematics at `t_{n+1}`, the state-dependent family is misaligned by one
step. For a harmonic load the resulting residual fraction is ≈ ωΔt. At full
scale, ω = 0.27–0.45 rad/s and Δt = 0.0707 s, giving a floor of **~3.2%** —
almost exactly the imbalance PLAN.md §8 names as the one that "still solves,
still produces plausible contour plots, and is still wrong."

**Resolution: align at export.** Each force is written at the index of the
state it was evaluated from — `state_force` at n, `external_force` at n+1 —
and the schema declares the convention per channel. The lag then disappears
rather than being budgeted for, and G4.1's tolerance is set by FE mapping
quality alone, which is what it should be measuring. The cost is one sample
at the end of the record.

**Guarded by G4.5, not by a tolerance.** The alignment fix is accepted over
declaring the floor, which means the fix itself now needs a guard: a
re-introduced lag would revert the residual's convergence *rate* to first
order while its absolute magnitude could still sit inside any tolerance that
had been set. G4.5 runs at Δt and Δt/2 and checks the rate. The rate is the
property that detects the regression; the magnitude is not.

### G4.1 inherits a second floor, and this one cannot be exported away

**Retraction.** This document previously stated that, with timestamp alignment
in place, "G4.1's tolerance is set by FE mapping quality alone". That is
withdrawn.

The `xi[3:6]` interpretation split (§10 Q2, E1) puts a **second, independent
floor** under G4.1, of order **0.4%** at the measured amplitude. Unlike the
`ωΔt` lag, it cannot be removed by re-indexing the export, because it is not a
bookkeeping error — the sources genuinely were computed under different
rotations.

The distinction between the two rung-4 gates is what makes this visible:

- **G1.6 stays clean.** It checks each source against *its own producer*, so
  each comparison is internally consistent and the split never appears.
- **G4.1 does not.** Equilibrium *sums* sources computed in disagreeing frames.
  The inconsistency has nowhere to go but the residual.

A gate that passes and a gate that cannot, on the same data, is the signature of
a real upstream defect rather than a mapping bug — and it is why G1.6 passing
must not be read as evidence that G4.1's residual is FloatFEA's fault.

**Quantify this floor properly before setting G4.1's tolerance.** The 0.4%
figure is a single-lever, single-axis estimate (E1); the floor that matters is
the residual of the *summed* load set over the real geometry, which requires the
FE model. Setting G4.1's tolerance before that measurement exists would either
pick something unachievable or — worse — pick a value above the floor, which is
exactly the level that hides a genuine load-path defect.

`docs/conventions.md` § Timestamps within a single RHS records the trap
itself, because it bites twice — once on export, and again on any channel
later added by analogy to an existing one.

## 7. Consequences for downstream milestones

F1 surfaced these; they are now adopted in PLAN.md and the verification
ladder, and are recorded here for traceability rather than restated.

**G3.1a / G3.1b** (PLAN.md §6 F3, ladder V3.1a / V3.1b). Mass reconciliation
is two gates because the buoys carry the entire floating mass, so structural
weight sets draft, which sets the hydrostatics and the BEM, which sets the
loads that size the structure. A single pass/fail gate placed across that loop
would never close. G3.1a is the code gate and blocks F4; G3.1b is the loop's
convergence criterion and blocks *trusting a result*, not building the
pipeline.

**Mass is frozen for the eight-week window** (PLAN.md §6 F3). Adopt a
structural estimate, put it into FloatSim, analyse against it, and iterate the
design in Phase 2. Every report states which iteration it belongs to and
whether G3.1b held for it. The residual risk is that the frozen estimate is far
enough off that the analysed loads are unrepresentative — G3.1b is what makes
that visible instead of silent. The number to freeze is Q3 in §10.

**G3.3** (ladder V3.3). Exact hollow-section formulae are binding on F3;
thin-wall approximations are order-check tools only, must be labelled, and must
use mean diameter. Origin is the error in §8.

**G4.4 — mapping conservation** (ladder rung 4). G1.6 validates the *export* —
that panel pressures integrate to the body force FloatSim applied. It says
nothing about transferring a 1488-panel hydro field onto an FE mesh that does
not share its topology. Mapped nodal loads must preserve total force **and
moment about a common point**, per body and per source. A panel field can pass
G1.6 and still be mapped wrongly; two failure modes, two gates.

**G4.5 — residual convergence rate** (ladder rung 4). Guards the §6 timestamp
alignment. See §6.

**The six rigid-body constraints are the only restraint, and that is now
load-bearing.** Confirmed by inspection: F4 never assumed mooring reactions
provide restraint — `PLAN.md:107` names mooring only as a *load* source
("connector and mooring loads are point loads at known attachment nodes"), and
the F4 description restrains the model solely through "Lagrange multipliers on
six rigid-body constraints". The design was correct as written.

What has changed is its status. With `/mooring` optional and **empty for this
platform** (`connections = 0`, G1.0 §4.2), those six constraints are no longer
one restraint among several — they are the *entire* restraint of the FE model.
Stated explicitly here rather than left implicit, because an implicit assumption
that becomes load-bearing is exactly the kind that survives a later change
unexamined. If a moored variant is ever analysed, the interaction between mooring
stiffness and the inertia-relief constraints must be reopened deliberately, not
absorbed.

The multipliers remain the diagnostic they were designed to be — but see
`docs/findings/mean-drift-and-screening.md`: the platform drifts in surge, and a
drifting rigid-body mode is precisely what inertia relief absorbs, so the
multipliers will carry that drift. They must be read with it in mind rather than
as evidence of a load-path error.

**The fin is not a beam problem.** A 21.5 m disc at ~195 mm cannot be
represented by the F2 space-frame solver at all. Including it as an FEA target
pulls CalculiX shell work out of F7's tail and into the core deliverable. The
plate-to-spar junction and the joint attachment are local shell problems on
the same footing.

## 8. Structural assumptions

Provisional. These belong in the F3 model-definition YAML and never in the
interchange schema — FloatSim has no structural properties and never will.

| | assumption | basis |
|---|---|---|
| Material, all structure | S355: E = 210 GPa, ν = 0.3, ρ = 7850 kg/m³, f_y = 355 MPa | offshore standard |
| Spar shell | D = 8.41 m, t = 35 mm (D/t = 240) | inside API RP 2A's D/t ≤ 300 |
| Heave plate | D = 21.5 m, t = 195 mm equivalent | geometric scale of the 3.9 mm hydrodynamic rim; likely a stiffened plate in reality |
| Cluster arm (hub→buoy) | 25 m span, 2.5 m × 180 mm tubular | order-checked below |
| Platform cross-truss arm | 50 m span, triangulated, depth undecided | see below |
| Plate-to-spar joint | rigid in the beam model | shell sub-model in F7 |
| Articulated joints | idealised kinematic constraint, no compliance | matches FloatSim's `yaw_locked` |

**Section properties are computed exactly, not by thin-wall approximation.**
An earlier draft of these figures used `W ≈ πD²t/4` on the outer diameter,
which overstates capacity by 20% and understates mass by 6%. The two errors
are in opposite directions, so the resulting demand/capacity pair looked
self-consistent and nothing flagged it. Exact hollow-section formulae are used
throughout below and are now binding on F3 as **G3.3**.

Every figure below states the assumed outer diameter, because an unstated
input is a number that cannot be re-checked — which is exactly how the
cluster-arm error survived its first pass.

Common inputs: `σ_allow = 0.6·f_y = 213 MPa`, `ρ_steel = 7850 kg/m³`,
`W = π(D_o⁴ − D_i⁴)/(32·D_o)`, `A = π(D_o² − D_i²)/4`.

**What the 604 t is (B1, resolved from source).** `platform_common.py:40-43`
gives `M_PER_BUOY − M_BUOY = 33.5033 − 28.67 = 4.8333 kg`, which equals
`(ARMS_TOTAL + PLATFORM_MASS)/12 = (48 + 10)/12` exactly. So the 604 t joint
force is **each buoy's share of the entire dry structure — rods and truss
together**, not of the buoy alone. Full-scale placeholder total dry mass is
7,250 t: 6,000 t rods/hubs plus 1,250 t truss.

The truss does hang off the cluster rods: the whole dry assembly is supported
only through the twelve buoy joints, so each rod carries a twelfth of it. That
part of the load path is right.

**Note the circularity.** 604 t is derived from the *placeholder* dry mass. Any
sizing done against it embeds the placeholder, and a lighter structure reduces
the joint force that sized it. This is the G3.1b design loop in miniature, and
it is the reason the frozen mass must be one internally consistent set rather
than components sized against different totals.

**Cluster arm.** The joint delivers 604 t = 5.93 MN at the rod tip. Over the
25 m arm that is **M = 148.2 MN·m**, so the demand is **W = 0.696 m³**.

At the assumed **D_o = 2.5 m**: a 150 mm wall gives `D_i = 2.2 m` and
`W = 0.614 m³` — **13% short**. Closing it needs t ≈ 175 mm, so 180 mm is the
buildable number: `D_i = 2.14 m`, `W = 0.710 m³`, `A = 1.312 m²`, **257 t**
over 25 m. Demand and capacity are then within 2%, and within 15% across the
plausible range. For an order-check that makes the section **plausible rather
than comfortable** — it should be re-examined, not treated as settled.

**Platform cross-truss — earlier figure retracted.** A previous draft of this
section put **M = 889 MN·m** at the truss centre by applying all three buoys'
net force (1,812 t) at 50 m, and derived a 5,780 t solid-tube bound and a
1,960 t truss from it. **That moment is wrong by 5.8×.** Most of the force is
consumed supporting the rods and hub *at the hub*; only the truss's own share
travels inward:

```
3 rods deliver up at hub    17.78 MN
rods + hub own weight      −14.71 MN
net onward to truss arm      3.07 MN = 313 t
  × 4 arms = 1,250 t  ≡  truss placeholder     [closure check]
moment at centre, one arm  = 153 MN·m
```

The ×4 closure onto the truss placeholder is what confirms the corrected
figure. Every number derived from 889 MN·m is withdrawn.

**B1 is the direct cause of the error, and the arithmetic closes it.**
`12 × 604.17 t = 7,250 t` exactly — the full placeholder dry mass. That
identity is what proves 604 t was the share of the *whole* dry structure
(rods **and** truss), and it is precisely why applying `3 × 604 t` at the 50 m
arm was wrong: the truss's own weight was already inside the 604 t, so carrying
it outward to the centre counted it a second time. Reading the 604 t as the
buoy's share of the *truss alone* would have made the original calculation
correct; it is not, and the ×12 closure is the check that settles it.

**And 153 MN·m is still not the design moment.** A four-arm symmetric cross
under symmetric load is self-equilibrated at the centre — there is no cantilever
root moment. The governing case is the **antisymmetric differential** between
clusters, which is dynamic. Per-cluster waterplane is 166.6 m² at full scale, so
relative heave costs `ρgA = 1.675 MN/m`, i.e. **83.8 MN·m per metre of
differential heave** at the 50 m lever. Design moment is order 150–300 MN·m, and
pinning it is exactly what the FE model exists to do — it cannot be settled by
statics here.

At 200 MN·m and 10 m truss depth the chord force is 20 MN, giving 0.094 m² per
chord, ~74 t per arm for two chords, ~295 t across four, and roughly **620 t**
with bracing and connection allowances. Against the **1,250 t** placeholder
(C4-c, "10 kg platform", itself flagged as an assumption in
`platform-geometry.md`).

So the placeholder is roughly twice a corrected first-pass sizing, not a
shortfall. The earlier "the budget is tight and swings by a factor of three"
conclusion followed from the retracted moment and does not hold.

What survives from the earlier draft is the *mechanism*, not the magnitude:
because the buoys carry the entire floating mass (S8), structural weight sets
draft, which sets the hydrostatics and the BEM, which sets the loads that size
the structure. That circularity is real regardless of the numbers, and it is
what motivates the G3.1a/G3.1b split and the frozen mass.

**The connector failure disappeared, and that is not a fix.**
`test_property_F_ref_equals_T_pullback_of_F_attach` genuinely fails for its

state than a red test, because nothing now signals it.

This also collapses the intended sequence of "tag with the failure present, then
fix on its own branch". The determinism fix and the failure's presence are
mutually exclusive: derandomizing is what makes the corner unreachable. Both
were required, so the failure could not survive into the tagged baseline.

**Disposal — decided, and it takes two tests, not one (H1).** Pinning the
failing example inside the test whose domain was narrowed would fail by
construction: the corner is excluded from that test's domain precisely because
it is out of scope. The disposal is therefore:

1. **The property test**, over the narrowed in-scope domain — arms and
   stiffnesses the attachment transform is actually required to serve. Green,
   and green for a stated reason.
2. **A separate documenting test**, asserting the *known degraded behaviour* at
   the out-of-scope corner (K ~ 1e8 against a 1 nm arm). It asserts what the
   transform does there, not that it is accurate.

The limit is then pinned and visible without being red, and — the part that
matters — **a change in that behaviour surfaces as a failure** rather than
passing unnoticed. A corner that is merely excluded is a corner nobody is
watching.

Own branch, deliberate re-measure of the baseline afterwards. Widening `rtol`
remains the wrong answer.

**Q1 — DECIDED: recompute hydrostatics in FloatFEA.** `p = ρg(η − z)` on the
wetted panels is closed-form and needs no hydro machinery. The decisive
argument is the §2 finding that gravity and hydrostatic cancel inside `C` at
ξ = 0 upstream: `C` is a restoring *derivative*, not a load, so there is no
pressure field inside it to extract and backing one out means inverting a
linearisation. Both sides are reconstructed independently — gravity from the FE
mass distribution, buoyancy from hull geometry. This also gives G4.3 real
content, since it becomes an independent check rather than a comparison against
the record.

Two conditions, now carried as **G4.6** in PLAN.md §6 F4:

- Linearise the recomputed buoyancy about ξ = 0 and compare the 6×6 against
  FloatSim's `C`, **per body**. Disagreement means hull geometry or waterplane
  differs between the two models, and it must surface there rather than as an
  unexplained G4.1 residual.
- Evaluate on the **mean** wetted surface, matching FloatSim's linearisation.
  The instantaneous surface adds a nonlinearity FloatSim never had, which would
  appear as a G4.1 residual with no obvious cause. A deliberate consistency
  choice — not to be "improved" later without reopening the gate.

**Q2 — What evidence establishes that FloatSim's θ is valid at the observed
max|θ|?**

*This question was previously posed as a displacement criterion at a lever arm
— O(θ²) stretch versus O(θ³) arc error at 50 m — and that framing was wrong on
both branches.* A rotation vector is axis-angle and therefore an **exact**
parameterisation. Both of those error terms are artifacts of the linearised
map `I + [θ×]`, not of the representation, and both vanish at zero cost under
Rodrigues reconstruction, which `docs/conventions.md` now mandates. Neither
number bounds anything once the map is correct.

What the linearised map actually does, and the reason the mandate exists, is
stretch every transformed vector by `√(1+θ²)` — 1.5% at 10°, 4.3% at 17° —
silently magnifying every transformed load. That failure is now prevented by
convention rather than by a bound.

The question that survives is **upstream**: FloatSim integrates with `xi[3:6]`
treated as small-angle linear Euler throughout, so the θ it reports is the
output of a linearised *dynamics*, valid only while that linearisation holds.
Rodrigues yields an exact rotation from whatever θ FloatSim reports; **it
cannot recover information the linearisation never carried.** So what evidence
establishes that θ itself is trustworthy at the observed amplitudes? Candidate
evidence, none of it a displacement criterion:

- orthogonality or energy drift accumulated over a full run;
- comparison of a case against a non-linearised (finite-rotation) integration;
- the existing `0.1 rad` figure in `multibody-conventions.md` Item 2, which
  bounds the *connector attachment transform*'s O(θ²) error at 0.5% — a
  narrower claim than whole-model dynamic validity, and it should not be
  borrowed as though it were the latter.

**Measured, and the question is live.** §9 records max‖θ‖ = 0.157 rad (9.0°) at
the rotational mode, converged — **1.6× the 0.1 rad figure**

> **Qualifier, and it is undischarged.** 8.97° is converged *conditional on the
> drift state at t ≈ 300 s*. The platform drifts in surge
> (`docs/findings/mean-drift-and-screening.md`), and drift velocity 0.0087 m/s is
> **1.6% of orbital velocity at a 2 m wave, 3.2% at 1 m** — so the drag
> perturbation is ~3% **and rising**. That is the same order as the 0.4%
> inter-module rotation inconsistency and the 3.2% ωΔt floor, both of which this
> project treated as significant. Rotation flat to 0.004% and position moving
> 3.2% per period are not in conflict: they are different quantities and only one
> has converged. **Quote 8.97° with this qualifier wherever it is relied on** —
> Q2 turns on it., and confined to
the buoys, with hubs and platform at ~0.02 rad. Off-resonance the platform is
at 0.39× the figure. So this cannot be waved through, and the evidence Q2 asks

**The buoy-confinement is not reassuring, and an earlier draft of this section
wrongly implied it was.** The question is not *where rotation is large* but
**where rotation enters the load path** — and it enters at the spars. Their
motion drives the hydrodynamics, and their rotation enters the transform that
puts connector reactions onto the truss. A rotation-accuracy defect on a body
that barely deforms still propagates into member forces on the body that does.
Confinement to the buoys localises the *cause*, not the *consequence*.

Note also that 1.6× is the **mildest relevant case**: steady state in a regular
wave. Irregular seas (Phase 2) produce higher peaks, so the exceedance only
grows from here.

**ESCALATED (E1): this is a FloatSim defect, not a conventions finding, and it
is now the primary evidence for LEVEL2-INTEGRATOR-UNWIRED.**

FloatSim interprets `xi[3:6]` three ways — ZYX-intrinsic Euler in `morison.py`,
axis-angle rotvec in `joints.py`, linearised rotation vector in
`hydrostatics.py` (`docs/conventions.md` § Rotations). Morison, joint and
hydrostatic loads all feed **the same equations of motion** under mutually
disagreeing rotations. So the dynamics are not self-consistent beyond first
order, at the amplitude every design case sits at.

That reframes the question. It is no longer *whether θ is valid at the observed
amplitude* — it is that **θ is not a single well-defined quantity there.** A
bound on one interpretation's error would not settle it, because the three
interpretations are simultaneously in force.

*Inputs behind the divergence figures, stated so they can be re-checked:*
rotation axis `(1, 1, 0.6)/‖·‖`; lever `v = (0, 0, −1.4574)` m, the submerged
spar length at model scale, applied as `‖R_rotvec·v − R_eulZYX·v‖`. At
‖θ‖ = 0.15657 rad this is **5.84 mm on a 1.4574 m lever = 0.401%**; at 0.30 rad,
19.31 mm = 1.325%. At full scale the same fractions apply to a 72.87 m lever, so
0.401% is **0.29 m**. The fraction is scale-invariant; the displacement is not.

**Scoped as a bounded experiment, not an architectural discussion.**
LEVEL2-INTEGRATOR-UNWIRED must not sit open on F1's critical path. Convert it to
a measurement: integrate the same case with an exact finite-rotation update for
`xi[3:6]` against the current linearised treatment, and compare max‖θ‖, pitch
natural period, and connector loads. Small differences → document the bound and
proceed. Large → a real FloatSim defect, and F1 then waits on a known thing
rather than an open question.

**Q3 — DECIDED (2026-08-10): freeze the placeholder set, 7,250 t total dry —
6,000 t rods/hubs + 1,250 t truss.**

**This is a scaling-consistency decision, not a sizing decision.** That
distinction is the most important thing in this entry. The number was chosen so
that the full-scale deck is an *exact geometric scale* of the model deck, not
because 1,250 t is the right amount of steel for the truss. A first-pass
corrected sizing gives ~620 t (§8), so the frozen figure is roughly twice what
structure would require. **In Phase 2 the reason to depart from 1,250 t will be
structural, and whoever does it must know that the original choice never was.**

Rationale:

- *It preserves exact Froude correspondence, which Q5 depends on.* The existing
  platform BEM was computed at `PLATFORM_DZ = 0.21638`, the draft corresponding
  to `M_PER_BUOY = 33.5033 kg`. Freezing the placeholder keeps the full-scale
  deck an exact scale of the model one, so Q5's cross-check remains **"must
  agree exactly"**. Freezing 1,400 t would shift draft by 0.22 m and degrade
  that to "agrees within the mass difference" — which forfeits the whole reason
  Q5 was worth running, for 150 t between two figures both ~2× a corrected
  sizing.
- *It is internally consistent.* The 604 t joint force derives from the 7,250 t
  placeholder. Freezing components sized against a different total makes the set
  self-contradictory.
- *The rejected alternative's derivation was invalid anyway.* 1,400 t came from
  tapering truss chords down from a 889 MN·m root moment, and that base moment
  is the one retracted in §8 as 5.8× too large. The tapering argument is sound
  in principle; the base it was applied to was not.

**G3.1b will fail against this frozen mass. That is correct behaviour.** The FE
model will compute a lighter structure than 7,250 t, and G3.1b exists precisely
to make that visible. **Do not reconcile it by adjusting the FE model to match
the frozen figure — that is backwards.** The frozen mass is an input to the
loads; the FE mass is an output of the design. G3.1b reports the gap; Phase 2
closes it by iterating the design and re-running the BEM.

Depth sensitivity (8 m → heavier, 12 m → lighter) is a real lever but does not
bear on this decision, since the choice is not a sizing one. Beyond steel the
trade is bracing length and hydrodynamic exposure, so depth should not increase
without establishing whether the truss is ever submerged.

*Precondition, carried unchanged:* KD-2 implies M/K off by ~45% in pitch.
Freezing a truss mass into a model carrying that defect freezes the defect with
it, and G3.1b then cannot separate the estimate from the upstream error. Bound
or resolve KD-2 before the freeze, or record explicitly that the frozen value
inherits it.
