### DY0. The members are STIFFNESS equivalents, and the mass splits by fraction

**Overridable default, recorded here because the first result rests on it.**

R587 found that the superstructure scope removed the load the one designed section was
sized for. The fix was ruled at DX0 — the buoys enter as joint reactions, not as mass —
and R591 then found a second thing wrong, in the sizing rather than the scope: the
model's centre of gravity sat **10.3315 m below** the one the deck declares, because
"size the members to reproduce the body's mass" made the members consume the whole body
and left nothing to sit at the CoG.

**"Size members to the body mass" is withdrawn.** The in-plane members are the
**stiffness** equivalent of a truss with depth, not a model of its steel:

```
claim  a member carrying its own steel cannot be right either
cmd    4 * 50 m * basis.tube_area(2.5, 0.180) * basis.RHO_STEEL
out    2059.7 t against the platform body's 1250.0 t deck mass -- 1.65x it
rule   DY0b asked for this figure confirmed or corrected; the directive's ~2060 t
       is 2059.7 t
```

**a) Section.** F1's recorded section for each member type, via
`Section.circular_tube`: `D_o = 2.5 m`, `t = 180 mm`, giving `A = 1.311929 m²`,
`I = 0.887979 m⁴`, `J = 1.775958 m⁴` (`docs/milestones/F1.md:389`, basis at
`F1.md:425-433`). **The GEOMETRY is what the gate asserts**, exactly, because the
derived figures above are those two numbers rounded to seven places and comparing
against them would need a transcription tolerance loose enough to hide a real section
change.

**b) Steel density is not used for member mass**, per the arithmetic above.

**c) The split, per body `b`**, with deck mass `M_b`, CoG `r_G` and inertia `J_b`
about `G` all read from `platform12_deck.yaml`:

* members carry `f · M_b` as a uniform line mass `μ_b = f · M_b / L_b`, through an
  equivalent density `ρ_eq,b = μ_b / A`. Rotary inertia follows as `ρ_eq · I`, which
  keeps the two consistent by construction;
* the remainder `m_r = (1 − f) · M_b` goes at
  `r_r = (M_b · r_G − Σ member first moments) / m_r`, a lumped mass joined to the
  body's centre node by a rigid link;
* its rotary inertia is `J_r = J_b(G) − J_mem(G) − m_r(|d|²·1 − d·dᵀ)`, `d = r_r − r_G`,
  with **`J_mem(G)` taken from the assembled member-only mass matrix** — not a rod
  formula, and about `G` rather than about the member centroid. Getting that reference
  point wrong cost a 1.07% error in the platform's inertia.

**Default `f = 0.5`:** a truss with its bottom chord in the joint plane carries about
half its mass there.

```
claim  f = 0.5 is admissible for all five bodies and lands where DY0c predicted
cmd    build_superstructure()
out    platform  f 0.5  rho_eq 2382.0 kg/m^3  m_r 625.0 t  r_r z 45.3315  link 20.6630 m
out    hub1..4   f 0.5  rho_eq 7622.4 kg/m^3  m_r 750.0 t  r_r z 24.6685  link  0.0000 m
rule   DY0c predicted r_r about 20.66 m above the joint plane and rho_eq about 2380
judge  the hub link is 0.0000 m because a hub's mass reference is already on the
       joint plane (DW1), so only the platform's remainder is offset
```

**d) Admissibility.** If `m_r < 0`, or `J_r` has an eigenvalue below `−tol`, lower `f`
for that body through `{0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0}` and take the largest admissible value.
`f = 0` is always admissible. **A lowered `f` is a SIZING FINDING**, reported with a
test that asserts the chosen value — not a HOLD trigger.

**e) `ρ_eq` is reported for every body, and anything above 7850 kg/m³ is a reported
finding.** None is today; the hubs at 7622.4 are close enough that `f = 0.6` would
exceed it.

**What this is NOT.** It is not a claim that the truss weighs half its mass in the
joint plane — it is a default that makes the model reproduce the deck's mass, CoG and
inertia simultaneously, which no single-density member set can. The `f` sensitivity
check (`f = 0.25` and `0.75`, one period, platform body) belongs to the member-force
table step, not to F3.

## 2. BUILDER LIMITS (verbatim)

```
L/D >= 2
L/r <= 300
```

**The builder REFUSES a member outside either limit.** Not a warning, not a
clamp: `CLAUDE.md` § Non-negotiables. `L/D >= 2` keeps the member in the range a
beam element describes at all — below it the cross-section is not slender against
the span and a space frame is the wrong model. `L/r <= 300` is the slenderness
ceiling Xabier set.

**Both limits are a refusal and neither is a tolerance.** They are properties of
the model the tool accepts, declared in the plan and asserted by the builder, and
they do not belong in `floatfea/tolerances.py` — nothing is compared against them
to decide whether a computed number is close enough to another.

**THEY ARE COMPUTED ON MEMBER LENGTH (DW3), AND FOR THIS FRAME THAT IS THE IN-PLANE
LENGTH.** §3.1 measures the frame as planar, so a member's length is its horizontal
run and not a reference-to-reference distance. On F1:389's recorded section
(`r = 0.822709 m`) both arm types clear both limits:

| member | L | L/D | L/r |
|---|---|---|---|
| hub arm, centre→hub | 50.000 m | 20.00 | 60.77 |
| cluster arm, hub→buoy joint | 25.000 m | 10.00 | 30.39 |

against `L/D >= 2` and `L/r <= 300`. **Neither is close to a limit, and that is worth
stating because it means the limits will not be what refuses this model** — if the
builder refuses, it will be for a reason the plan has not anticipated, which is the
case to read carefully rather than to tune around.

### 3.3 BODY MASS BY RIGID LINK (DW2)

* **Member mass:** each member carries its own section mass.
* **Remainder:** each MODELLED body's remaining mass — deck mass minus member mass —
  goes on a lumped mass at the deck's reference point, with the remaining inertia
  about that point, attached to the nearest structural node by a V2.6 rigid link. The
  rigid links shipped in F2. **"Each modelled body" is the platform and the four
  hubs.** The twelve buoys are not bodies in this model and carry no lumped mass;
  §3.5 says why, and it is not a simplification.
* **The hubs need no link.** Their mass reference is already on the joint plane
  (§3.1), so the remainder attaches at the hub node and the link is zero-length.
  Only the platform's is offset, by 10.3315 m.
* **G3.1a:** assert the FE body's total mass, CoG and inertia tensor against the deck,
  **per body, never on the sum.**
* **If a member outweighs its body:** report it as a **sizing finding**, not a clamp
  to zero. A negative remainder is information about the section, and clamping it
  would make G3.1a pass on a body whose steel does not fit inside its own mass.

### 3.4 SCOPE: FIVE BODIES, AND THE BUOYS ARE LOADS RATHER THAN MASS (DW0, DX0)

The first result is **the platform body and the 4 hub bodies, 16 members.** The 12
buoy **spar columns** are **deferred until after the member-force table** as
*members* — they carry distributed wave loading and checking them is real work.

**THE BUOYS' LOADS ARE NOT DEFERRED, AND R587 WAS RIGHT THAT THIS NEEDED SAYING.**
The finding was that the buoys are 85.57% of the deck's mass and that the one
designed section — the 25 m cluster arm — was sized in `docs/milestones/F1.md:425-433`
from a 5.93 MN buoy tip load. A model that omitted that load would check the arm
against loads it was not sized for, and would look comfortable doing it.

**AND THE FIX IS NOT TO PUT THE BUOY MASSES IN, WHICH WOULD DOUBLE-COUNT (DX0).**
Each buoy is its own FloatSim body, joined to its hub by a gimbal. Everything the buoy
experiences — weight, buoyancy, wave force, its own inertia — reaches the cluster-arm
tip as the force and locked-axis moment that joint transmits, and FloatSim computes
exactly that. The reaction *already contains* the buoy's inertia. Adding a lumped buoy
mass beside it would count each buoy twice, once through the reaction and once through
the mass.

So this is DJ0 applied as written, not a reduced model: **the superstructure carries
the buoys' full loads through the joints**, and only the spar columns go unchecked as
members.

**Label, and it is narrower than DW0's first wording:** *buoy spar columns not
assessed as members; buoy loads applied as joint reactions.*

**G3.1a is asserted on the 5 modelled bodies against the deck.** That is correct
rather than a gap: the gate is per body, and a row reading "85.57% of deck mass
absent" would describe a quantity nobody checks.

**G4.1 closes per body with every joint reaction on it included**, and that is what
proves the arm sees its design action.

**DESIGN OBSERVATION, RECORDED RATHER THAN TREATED AS A MODELLING DEFECT.** The
16-member frame is a **tree**: 17 nodes, 16 members, nothing joining adjacent hubs and
nothing joining adjacent buoy joints, so there is no triangulation and no redundancy,
and the name "X-brace" describes bracing the deck does not contain. Each FloatSim body
is its own free-free structural model balanced by its own inertia relief — the
platform with 4 hub arms and its own mass, each hub a tripod of 3 cluster arms loaded
by its 3 buoy-joint reactions and its platform-joint reaction — so each is statically
determinate and self-equilibrating, which is correct. A horizontal planar tree under
vertical joint loads is a grillage: axial force is identically zero and every member
resists in bending and torsion, which is the action F1 sized the section for
(`M = 148.2 MN·m`). **The absence of cross-bracing is an observation about the
platform, and it belongs in the member-force table's commentary rather than in the
model.**

### 3.5 THE JOINT REACTIONS, AND WHERE THEY COME FROM (DX1)

**So the work is ADDITIVE on the HSP side** — a writer that emits `res.lam`, and the
per-body external force beside it — and touches neither the integrator nor the force
model, which is what `CLAUDE.md` § Relationship to HSP requires. **F4's first item is
that export, not a reconstruction from equilibrium.**

**What still has to be checked once the export exists**, and it is the first test F4
inherits: each buoy's reaction equals that buoy's external force minus its `M·a` from
the same export, to round-off. It cannot be checked today, because the external force
is not exported for any body.

**One convention travels with the numbers and the reader already says so.**
`floatfea/io/reader.py:378-384` rejects a record carrying `lam` without
`jacobian_evaluation`, because `lam` is expressed against constraint-Jacobian rows in
each joint's own basis and not as a force/moment 6-vector. The locked-axis moment is a
**scalar magnitude along `R_A · ẑ`**, so reconstructing a moment vector needs the axis
— which `docs/platform-joints.md` measures and records per joint.

**Their return condition, written down so the deferral is a selection and not a
drift:** strip loads per buoy from F1's export, as vertical members from joint to
keel. The geometry is already measured — each buoy's reference point is **84.45 m
below** its joint point at full scale. Recorded in `docs/milestones/F2a.md`.

**Every output from the first result carries `buoy spar columns not assessed as
members; buoy loads applied as joint reactions` on its face**, for the same reason the
`wave-only` label exists: a utilisation that omits a load path and does not say so is
the failure mode this project exists to prevent.

**The label is narrower than DW0's first wording, deliberately.** No load path is
omitted, so "superstructure only; buoy spars not assessed" would overstate the gap and
invite a reader to discount a result that is complete in the loads it applies. What is
omitted is the check on the columns as members, and the label says that.

### R486's gate, made concrete

**What it replaces.** R486 asked for the rigid-mode ceiling shown valid across
the whole *admissible* domain. DD0 moved it out of F2: "every admissible
configuration" is an open-ended domain and each round found one more axis of it.
Asserting the ceiling on the platform's own members is a **stronger statement
about the structure being analysed and a weaker one about structures in general**,
and the trade is deliberate.

**What it asserts, per member:**

```
element_rigid_residual(k_local, L) <= RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS
```

using the element-local form F2 step 6 shipped as a diagnostic — six rigid
motions about the element's own midpoint, homogenisation on the matrix, so
orientation, span and reference point leave the quantity by construction.

**And its three counters go back into the injection guard.** F2 step 6 took the
residual pair out of `tests/test_counters_are_injected.py` because the gate it
defended had become a diagnostic; here it is an assertion again, so
`dropped_flip`, `wrong_dof_index` and `rotational_block` are registered against
it. `scripts/rigid_counter_response.py` measures what each reddens, and **the
count of elements a counter fails to reach is a number to take at the commit, not
to carry forward** — F2 saw that figure move three times with no code change.

**WHAT THIS GATE IS MEASURED ON — RE-LOCKED (EB0).** This section said "A platform
frame is mostly near-vertical members, so this gate is measured there first." **That
is false of this platform, and § 3.1 of this document already measured it so.** The
frame is planar: the band the instruction named is empty, so written as specified the
measurement had no subject at all, which is the recorded empty-parameter-set shape
rather than a gate. R486's original finding stands as the reason the element-local
form replaced the assembled one — a defect-free element reading `2.9994e-14` against
`1.068e-16` at 2.87° from vertical, on a *retired assembled* form — but it is not
reachable on this frame and this gate does not pretend to reach it.

**G2.1 is measured on the sixteen members the platform has.** Every figure below is

## 7. TOLERANCES THIS MILESTONE DECLARES

F2's table is in `docs/milestones/F2.md` and stays there; a tolerance declared in
F3 is stated here, which is what EG0(a) asks and what C40 and C60 were the cost of
not having.

| tolerance | value | what it bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `PLATFORM_RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS` | `PLATFORM_RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS = 1.154338e-18` | G2.1's element-local rigid residual, on the sixteen members of the shipped platform. The geometric centre of the window those members leave: clean worst `3.528257e-19`, weakest counter response `3.776640e-18`, width `10.70x`, balanced `3.27x` either side. **Not** the builder's ceiling -- see below. |
| `PLATFORM_RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS_COUNTER_DEFECT` | `PLATFORM_RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS_COUNTER_DEFECT = 1.0e-14` | The size the three counters inject at, and the smallest defect the gate must still fail. The same value as `RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS_COUNTER_DEFECT` deliberately -- EG0 reuses the declared injection rather than inventing one -- and it clears the binding per-member edge `3.088842e-15` by `3.24x`. |

**TWO SUBJECTS, TWO CEILINGS, AND ONE CONSTANT CANNOT SERVE BOTH (EG0(e)).**

* **The gate's subject is these sixteen members.** It asserts what is true of the
  platform being analysed, and its ceiling has to sit above every clean reading and
  below every counter response so that the three registered counters can redden it.
  `PLATFORM_RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS` is that value.
* **The refusal's subject is every deck a reader could write.** `check_rigid_modes`
  keeps `RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS = 1e-15`, which sits `51.1x` inside the clean worst
  over the admissible band and refuses no legal member anywhere measured.
* **The window over the admissible band is EMPTY.** Over 1680 admissible
  `(D_o, t/D_o, L)` points the clean worst is `5.287607e-18` and the weakest
  counter response is `1.571633e-19` -- the floor is already ABOVE the roof,
  because clean and counter responses move in opposite directions with
  slenderness. So no single value can both pass every legal member and be crossed
  by every counter, and **no counter is registered against the refusal.**

That asymmetry is the whole of R624's answer: a ceiling measured on another
quantity may not host the counters, and the gate's ceiling may not be stretched to
cover a subject on which no such ceiling exists.
