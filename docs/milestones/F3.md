## 2. BUILDER LIMITS (verbatim)

```
L/D >= 2
L/r <= 300
```

**The builder REFUSES a member outside either limit.** Not a warning, not a
clamp: `CLAUDE.md` § Non-negotiables. `L/D >= 2` keeps the member in the range a
beam element describes at all — below it the cross-section is not slender against
the span and a space frame is the wrong model. `L/r <= 300` is the slenderness
ceiling Xabier set.

**Both limits are a refusal and neither is a tolerance.** They are properties of
the model the tool accepts, declared in the plan and asserted by the builder, and
they do not belong in `floatfea/tolerances.py` — nothing is compared against them
to decide whether a computed number is close enough to another.

**THEY ARE COMPUTED ON MEMBER LENGTH (DW3), AND FOR THIS FRAME THAT IS THE IN-PLANE
LENGTH.** §3.1 measures the frame as planar, so a member's length is its horizontal
run and not a reference-to-reference distance. On F1:389's recorded section
(`r = 0.822709 m`) both arm types clear both limits:

| member | L | L/D | L/r |
|---|---|---|---|
| hub arm, centre→hub | 50.000 m | 20.00 | 60.77 |
| cluster arm, hub→buoy joint | 25.000 m | 10.00 | 30.39 |

against `L/D >= 2` and `L/r <= 300`. **Neither is close to a limit, and that is worth
stating because it means the limits will not be what refuses this model** — if the
builder refuses, it will be for a reason the plan has not anticipated, which is the
case to read carefully rather than to tune around.

### 3.1 THE FRAME IS PLANAR AT JOINT ELEVATION (DW1), and it was measured first

DW1 made the geometry conditional on two measurements. Both were taken before this
section was rewritten, and the ruling holds:

```
claim  all 16 joint points are coplanar
cmd    the exported deck, joint attach points resolved through each body's reference
out    z spread max − min = 0.000e+00 m, at z = 24.6685 m full scale
rule   a planar frame requires one joint elevation, exactly, not approximately
claim  the platform body's reference point is OFF that plane and the hubs' are ON it
out    platform  z = 0.700000 model → 10.3315 m full ABOVE the joint plane
out    hub1..4   z = 0.4933695679797303 model → exactly the joint plane
claim  the in-plane members are exactly 50 m and 25 m at full scale
out    centre→hub 50.0000 m; hub→buoy-joint 25.0000 m, dz = 0.000e+00
```

**So the 51.056 m member inclined 11.67° was the platform end alone**, and it is the
vector from the platform body's *mass reference* to the joint — not a structural
member. The frame is planar.

**AND THE HUBS NEED NO RIGID LINK AT ALL**, which DW2 did not assume: their mass
reference is already on the joint plane, so a hub's remainder mass attaches directly
at its node and the link is zero-length.

**WHAT THE DECK CANNOT TELL US, recorded as a forced assumption rather than a
choice.** `docs/load-interchange-v1.md:66` enumerates
`inertia_reference_point: "reference_point" | "cog"`. The 12-buoy deck declares
neither and carries exactly one point per body, with `mass` and `inertia` both
referred to it. So the reference point *is* the mass reference because nothing else
is declared — not because DW2 decided it is. `CLAUDE.md` § Non-negotiables requires a
transformation or assumption in use to be recorded in `assumptions` and surfaced in
the run log: **"body mass and inertia are taken at the deck's `reference_point`; the
deck declares no `cog` and no `inertia_reference_point`."**

**G3.1a is the gate that proves the sizing**, per body and never on the sum.

### 3.3 BODY MASS BY RIGID LINK (DW2)

* **Member mass:** each member carries its own section mass.
* **Remainder:** each body's remaining mass — deck mass minus member mass — goes on a
  lumped mass at the deck's reference point, with the remaining inertia about that
  point, attached to the nearest structural node by a V2.6 rigid link. The rigid links
  shipped in F2.
* **G3.1a:** assert the FE body's total mass, CoG and inertia tensor against the deck,
  **per body, never on the sum.**
* **If a member outweighs its body:** report it as a **sizing finding**, not a clamp
  to zero. A negative remainder is information about the section, and clamping it
  would make G3.1a pass on a body whose steel does not fit inside its own mass.

### R486's gate, made concrete

**What it replaces.** R486 asked for the rigid-mode ceiling shown valid across
the whole *admissible* domain. DD0 moved it out of F2: "every admissible
configuration" is an open-ended domain and each round found one more axis of it.
Asserting the ceiling on the platform's own members is a **stronger statement
about the structure being analysed and a weaker one about structures in general**,
and the trade is deliberate.

**What it asserts, per member:**

```
element_rigid_residual(k_local, L) <= RIGID_MODE_EXACTNESS
```

using the element-local form F2 step 6 shipped as a diagnostic — six rigid
motions about the element's own midpoint, homogenisation on the matrix, so
orientation, span and reference point leave the quantity by construction.

**And its three counters go back into the injection guard.** F2 step 6 took the
residual pair out of `tests/test_counters_are_injected.py` because the gate it
defended had become a diagnostic; here it is an assertion again, so
`dropped_flip`, `wrong_dof_index` and `rotational_block` are registered against
it. `scripts/rigid_counter_response.py` measures what each reddens, and **the
count of elements a counter fails to reach is a number to take at the commit, not
to carry forward** — F2 saw that figure move three times with no code change.

**What F3 must check that F2 could not.** The near-vertical band. R486's original
finding was a defect-free element reading `2.9994e-14` against `1.068e-16` at
2.87° from vertical on a *retired assembled* form. The element-local form reads
0.007–0.035 eps in that band. A platform frame is mostly near-vertical members,
so this gate is measured there first.
