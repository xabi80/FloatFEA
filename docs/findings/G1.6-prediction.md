# G1.6 — expected result, written BEFORE the first run

**Written:** 2026-08-14, before any G1.6 number exists.
**Purpose:** this is the last moment at which a prediction can be made that is
not a rationalisation. Everything below is committed in advance.

---

## Why a per-source prediction and not one threshold

A single tolerance across all sources would be satisfied by the wrong physics.
The three sources reach the record by **different routes**, and their expected
residuals differ by orders of magnitude for reasons that are known now. Writing
one number would hide that, and would let a radiation residual of zero — which
would be a *broken test* — read as an unusually good pass.

## The predictions

### Drag → round-off

**Expected: exactly 0.0, or floating-point round-off.**

Strip and patch loads are **re-evaluated, not reconstructed**. Module 2 already
demonstrated this against `make_morison_state_force`, the closure the integrator
itself calls: max deviation **exactly 0.0**. Nothing about assembling those same
values into a record changes that.

A non-zero drag residual would mean the export lost or reordered something
between evaluation and storage — a plumbing fault, not a physics one.

### Excitation → round-off

**Expected: round-off.**

Excitation is driven by the **incident wave**, which is a pure regular sinusoid.
Fundamental-only reconstruction of a single-frequency drive is exact, so the
coefficient form loses nothing. **The body's nonlinear response does not enter
here** — the incident field is prescribed, not responsive, and that is the whole
reason this source is clean while radiation is not.

### Radiation → NON-ZERO, concentrated at harmonics

**Expected: a non-zero residual with its energy at 2ω and 3ω.**

Radiation is driven by the **body motion**, which is provably non-sinusoidal:
quadratic drag (`u|u|`) and the nonlinear joint constraints both inject harmonic
content. A linear transfer function responds to that content at `B(2ω)`,
`B(3ω)`, and so on.

The two sides of the comparison then differ by construction:

| side | carries harmonics? |
|---|---|
| `mu`, exported directly in the time domain | **yes** — it is the convolution the solver applied |
| panel field, reconstructed at the fundamental | **no** — one frequency by construction |

**The residual *is* the harmonic content.** It is not an error to be driven down;
it is the measurement of how much of the radiation force lives above the
fundamental.

## S2 — the gate must be able to fail in BOTH directions

This is the part most easily lost once a number appears.

> **A radiation residual of zero is a BROKEN TEST, not a perfect
> reconstruction.**

The harmonics cannot have vanished — drag is quadratic and the joints are
nonlinear, so the motion is not a sinusoid and a fundamental-only reconstruction
*must* miss something. Zero means the comparison is not comparing what it claims
to: most likely both sides reconstructed the same way, or the spectral
decomposition is collapsing before it is read.

> **A non-trivial excitation residual is a REAL FINDING, to be chased rather
> than absorbed.**

The prediction above says it should be round-off. If it is not, the leading
candidate is that FloatSim evaluates excitation at the **instantaneous** rather
than the **mean** position — which the surge drift would then modulate through
the changing `kx` phase, since the platform translates ~2.3 spar diameters over
a run. That would be a genuine finding about the load path, and it would connect
two investigations that currently look separate.

Neither outcome is to be met by widening a tolerance.

## S3 — the record must be long enough to trip the panel validity bound

The validity bound is currently exercised **only against a mutated fixture**.
That tests that the rejection fires; it does not test that it fires *on real
data*, which is a different claim.

**The first G1.6 record should therefore be the drift case**, which moves the
platform ~2.3 spar diameters — comfortably past any sensible bound.

> If the bound does **not** trip when the recorded displacement says it should,
> that is a **finding about the bound**, not a clean pass.

A rejection that has never fired outside a test fixture is one you have tested,
not one you have seen work — the same distinction as the argument-versus-
measurement rule in `WORKFLOW.md`.

## Scale caveat, carried

The first G1.6 pass will be on a **model-scale** record; the full-scale re-run is
deferred to F3. F1's closure artifact carries the obligation to **re-run G1.6 on
the first full-scale record**, because a gate whose only passing evidence sits at
a scale that is not the target should be exercised once at the target. The
panel validity bound compounds this: being a displacement, it is scale-specific
and a model-scale bound does not transfer.

**Distinguishing them needs a longer record**, run to `~Q` cycles rather than

Recorded as inconclusive rather than resolved either way.

# V1 DISCRIMINATOR APPLIED — the residual is at the FORCING frequency

The transient and a convention error sit at different frequencies, so they can be
separated categorically rather than by waiting for decay:

```
settling transient      w_rot = 1.9291 rad/s   (T = 3.257 s)
phase-convention error  w_f   = 2.0004 rad/s   (T = 3.141 s)
separation              0.0712 rad/s = 3.56%
```

At this window length peak-picking cannot resolve them, so the test is a
least-squares **projection** onto each candidate rather than an FFT peak:

```
window 25.12 s = 8.0 cycles, worst hydro DOF 46

hypothesis                amplitude     explained var
mode    w_rot=1.9291    7.792155e-02          0.7566
forcing w_f  =2.0004    9.044709e-02          0.9992

joint fit   |mode| 3.440624e-03    |forcing| 9.340632e-02
joint design-matrix condition number 3.9
```

**The residual sits at the forcing frequency**: 99.92% explained, and the joint
fit puts **27× more amplitude** there. The condition number of 3.9 says the two
columns are well separated over this window — 8 cycles at 3.56% detuning is ~103°
of relative phase drift — so the joint fit is trustworthy rather than
ill-conditioned.

## But V1's discriminator needs one refinement, found by running it

A transient has **two** parts, and only one of them is at the mode:

| transient component | frequency |
|---|---|
| free decay of the rotational mode | `w_rot` — **excluded** by this result |
| driven response *building* toward steady state | **`w_f`** — not excluded |

So this result **rules out the free-decay transient** and does **not** rule out a
still-building driven response, which lives at the forcing frequency and would
masquerade as a convention error under this test alone.

That is a genuine gap in the discriminator as proposed, and it does not weaken
the recommendation — it sharpens what each half buys:

- **Frequency separation** cleanly excludes the *free* transient, categorically,
  and it has already done so.
- **Settling** is still required to exclude the *driven* build-up, because that
  component is only distinguishable by its amplitude ceasing to change.

The ~84-cycle re-run therefore remains right, but for a better-stated reason:
**it is not that frequency converges faster than decay — it is that the two
discriminators exclude different things and both are needed.**

## Status after V1: narrowed, still not resolved

Excluded: free-decay transient at the mode. Remaining: phase-convention error,

The first two are the K2 table's causes; the third is the one this record cannot

# W1 — BUILD-UP EXCLUDED BY AMPLITUDE DEPENDENCE, within the existing record

The third cause separates from the other two by **amplitude dependence**, not
frequency, so no re-run was needed:

```
convention / incomplete reference  ->  residual ~ A(t)     tracks the response
unsettled driven build-up          ->  residual ~ dA/dt    AM sidebands
```

The response envelope was **measured from the record**, not assumed exponential —
an assumed envelope would import the model under test.

```
worst hydro DOF 46, 15 envelope windows over 100.0 s

response envelope  A:  7.22063e-02 -> 6.46915e-02   (x0.90)
its derivative dA/dt: +2.49373e-03 -> -6.83091e-05
residual envelope  R:  9.66471e-02 -> 8.98146e-02   (x0.93)

hypothesis                              R^2
residual ~ A(t)    (convention)       0.9834
residual ~ dA/dt   (build-up)         0.0198

correlation A vs dA/dt: +0.0314
```

**The residual tracks the response amplitude, not its derivative.** R² 0.98
against 0.02, and the two regressors are near-orthogonal over this window
(correlation +0.03), so the test genuinely discriminates rather than being
degenerate — the same check that the joint fit's condition number provided for
V1.

**A second, independent observation points the same way.** The response envelope
on this DOF is *not building*: it falls slightly (×0.90) and `dA/dt` has already
crossed to near-zero. The Q≈134 settling estimate is for the **rotational mode**;
this DOF's response at the forcing frequency is driven rather than resonant and
settles far faster. So "the record is half-settled" was true of the mode and
misleading about the quantity actually being compared.

# X1/X2 — CONVENTION EXCLUDED ON MAGNITUDE. It is an incomplete reference.

Normalising the residual, and splitting it into magnitude and phase, closes the
two-way:

```
  DOF   |T| applied   |R-T|/|T|   |R|/|T|   arg R-T (deg)
   36  4.604659e-01      0.1778    1.0346          -9.84
   60  4.528439e-01      0.1711    1.0322          -9.49
   66  4.425047e-01      0.1811    1.0249         -10.16
   40  4.336900e-01      0.2147    1.0380         -11.90

magnitude-weighted |R-T|/|T| = 0.2085
magnitude-weighted |R|/|T|   = 1.0223
magnitude-weighted phase     = -11.07 deg
```

**Conventions are discrete. This is not one.**

| convention | predicted `|R-T|/|T|` |
|---|---|
| 180° sign flip | 2.000 |
| 90° (`e^{-iwt}` vs `e^{+iwt}`) | 1.414 |
| **measured** | **0.209** |

0.209 inverts to an **11° rotation**, and *there is no convention that rotates by
eleven degrees*. The convention candidate is excluded on magnitude alone —
without needing the spectral argument at all.

**The second reading agrees and adds to it.** A pure phase error preserves
magnitude exactly, `|R|/|T| = 1`. Measured: **1.0223** — close to 1, but
**strictly greater**, which is the signature of a **missing term in quadrature**
with the dominant one. That is what an incomplete reference looks like, and it is
not what a rotation looks like.

## What the reference is missing

The prediction is built from the **tabulated** `A(ω)`, `B(ω)`; `mu` is the
convolution against the **finite, truncated, resampled retardation kernel**.
Those are not the same object:

- the kernel is truncated at 60 s (6001 lags);
- it is built from a 79-point ω grid with contaminated slices excluded;
- the convolution is a left-endpoint sum, which module 1 measured converging to
  the continuous result at **first order** in `dt`.

Each is small; together they are an O(10–20%) discrepancy in a quantity where
`B` itself is small relative to `A`. **This is a property of the comparison, not
a defect in either side** — the kernel is a legitimate approximation of the
tabulated data, and `mu` is exactly what the solver applied (verified
bit-identical).

## G1.6 radiation — RESOLVED

| candidate | status |
|---|---|
| free-decay transient at the mode | excluded — V1, residual at `w_f` |
| unsettled driven build-up | excluded — W1, tracks `A(t)` not `dA/dt` |
| phase-convention error | **excluded — X1, 0.209 is not a discrete convention** |
| **incomplete reference** | **confirmed — X2, `|R|/|T| = 1.022 > 1`, quadrature** |

The S1 prediction was wrong about *where* the radiation residual would sit, and
the reason is now understood: it assumed the comparison would be against an exact
frequency-domain reference, when the available reference is a truncated kernel
approximation whose own error exceeds the harmonic content the prediction was
looking for. The harmonics are there — 3.4e-4 at 2ω — they are simply two orders
below the reference error.

**Consequence for the gate — and a guard against the tempting wrong fix.**

The clean-looking move is to remove the kernel from *both* sides: compare the
panel-integrated field directly against tabulated `A(ω)`, `B(ω)`. That makes
G1.6 a pure panel test **and guts it** — it would no longer validate against what
FloatSim *applied*, which is the entire reason the gate exists.

> **The kernel appears on both sides or neither, and "neither" is not available
> without losing the gate's purpose.**

So the correct fix is to push the **panel side through the same kernel**, not to
lift the kernel off the `mu` side.

The quantitative confirmation and its upstream consequences are recorded
separately in `docs/findings/kernel-vs-tabulated-damping.md`: the purely-
quadrature fit agrees to **1.85%**, and the discrepancy is **206% of the
tabulated `B` contribution** — which matters because the response was already
measured to be radiation-damping-controlled rather than drag-controlled.

# PRE-REGISTERED — the stationary-record test, written BEFORE it is run

**Written:** 2026-08-16, with no stationary record in existence.

The window-mismatch resolution says: `mu(t)` convolves 60 s of history while the
comparison window is 25 s, and the response envelope is not stationary over the
longer one. The test is to repeat on a record stationary over a full kernel
memory.

## The mechanism's own estimate falls short

```
envelope x0.90 over 100 s   ->  tau_env = 949 s
kernel memory 60 s          ->  tau_K / tau_env = 6.32%  =  3.6 deg
implied residual            ~   0.063
observed                        0.209
                                SHORT BY 3.3x
```

The estimate is crude — effective memory weight, non-uniform kernel, local
envelope slope all unmodelled — but it is short by enough that **the test must be
read quantitatively, not as pass/fail.**

## AC1 — THE ENVELOPE IS FITTED, AND THE MECHANISM IS TWO ORDERS SHORT

The crude "×0.90 over 100 s" reading is superseded by a fit of
`A(t) = A_inf + dA·exp(-t/tau)`:

```
 DOF        A_inf           dA    tau (s)  dA/A_end   60s var
  40  6.32734e-02  2.48209e-02       24.5    0.0094    0.860%
  46  6.38979e-02  2.23351e-02       27.8    0.0131    1.158%
   2, 20                          fit diverged -- reported, not used
```

**This is Case B: settling toward a non-zero asymptote**, with `tau ≈ 25–28 s`,
not the 949 s the crude reading implied. Case A (pure decay, in which no record
length ever becomes stationary and the re-run would fail its own premise) is
excluded.

Consequence for the mechanism's size:

| basis | 60 s variation | implied residual | short by |
|---|---|---|---|
| crude ×0.90/100 s (AB1 as first written) | 6.32% | 0.063 | 3.3× |
| **fitted** | **0.86 – 1.16%** | **~0.010** | **~20×** |

**The window mismatch can account for at most ~5% of the observed 0.209.** It is
not the explanation, and the pre-registration below is revised accordingly —
before the run, per AC2, so the thresholds sit against a fitted prediction rather
than a hope.

*Side note the fit settles:* `tau ≈ 25 s` means these DOF are ~4 time-constants
settled at the end of the record, i.e. ~98%. The "half-settled" figure came from
`Q ≈ 134` on the **rotational mode**; these DOF are driven, not resonant, and
settle far faster. That was flagged qualitatively under W1 and is now quantified.

## Both outcomes, committed in advance

| outcome | reading |
|---|---|
| **Residual essentially unchanged**, 0.209 → ~0.20 | **THE EXPECTED OUTCOME** given the fit. The mechanism is worth ~0.01 and removing it should barely move the number. The contradiction stands and remains unexplained. |
| **Partial collapse**, 0.209 → ~0.19–0.20 | Same reading as above — this *is* the predicted size of the effect, not a partial success. |
| **FULL collapse** to round-off | **Surprising, and not confirmation.** The fitted mechanism cannot account for 0.209, so a full collapse means something *else* changed between the two runs and must be identified before anything is concluded. |

**A partial collapse is not a success — and after AC1 it is not even a surprise.**
The mechanism's fitted size is ~5% of the residual. Reading a ~5% reduction as
closure would be the failure this pre-registration exists to prevent, and it is
now the *predicted* result rather than a hypothetical one.

## AC3 — the test needs less than a stationary record

The requirement is **not** a stationary whole record. It is **60 s of flat
history immediately preceding the comparison window**, with the comparison window
itself a few cycles. That is what the convolution actually reaches back through,
and it is a much weaker condition — the re-run should be designed to it rather
than to whole-record stationarity.

## Constraint on future diagnosis (AB2)

`ξ̈ = iω·ξ̇` at a single frequency, so **`dA` and `dB` are perfectly confounded**
and no amount of care separates them from single-frequency data. The degenerate
fit — `dB/B_tab` of 46.9, 44.6, 39.2, 6233 — is the degeneracy announcing itself,
not a failed attempt.

This is a **permanent structural limit on single-frequency diagnosis**, not a
property of this harness. Only multi-frequency data separates them: Q5's
model-scale cross-check, or a small frequency sweep, would supply it. Any future
attempt to fit `dA` and `dB` on one frequency should be rejected at design time.

# AH1/AH2 — G1.6's RADIATION DESIGN, PINNED BEFORE THE RE-SYNC

Everything above this line was written while the residual was unexplained. It is
now explained: the convolution's `k=0` endpoint
(`docs/findings/radiation-convolution-endpoint.md`), fixed upstream at HSP
`9fb5b33` with a permanent gate at `dccbecb`.

That closure creates a **design choice the pre-registration does not currently
resolve**, and it must be resolved *before* the `.flr` is re-synced, not after
the number comes back.

## The two live predictions

Both are legitimate, and they differ by two orders of magnitude:

| design | what the panel prediction is built from | predicted residual |
|---|---|---|
| **tabulated** | `A(ω)`, `B(ω)` straight from the BEM database | **~0.049** — trapezoid against exact quadrature. **Real and permanent.** |
| **AF3 — solver's own** | the solver's discrete convolution, same rule on both sides | **round-off** |

**Mixing them is a trap in both directions.** A round-off result read against an
expectation of 0.049 looks like an anomaly worth chasing. A 0.049 result read
without a design decision on record looks like a residual worth chasing. Neither
is true, and which mistake gets made would be decided by whichever number arrives
first — exactly the situation pre-registration exists to prevent.

## DECISION: AF3

**G1.6's radiation prediction is built through the solver's own discrete
convolution, and its target is round-off.**

Two reasons, in order:

1. **It measures the right thing.** With the same quadrature on both sides, the
   quadrature cancels and G1.6 measures **panel reconstruction alone** — which is
   the gate's actual purpose. The tabulated design measures panel reconstruction
   *plus* a known, already-quantified quadrature difference, and reports their
   sum as one number.

2. **The 0.049 design leaves a permanent expected residual**, and every future
   reader of this gate would have to be told that 5% is fine. **"Expected
   residuals" are how gates go soft.** A gate whose pass condition is "about 5%,
   but we know why" cannot distinguish a new 5% defect from the old one. Round-off
   can.

The `~0.049` does not disappear — it stays a measured property of trapezoid
against exact quadrature, recorded in `radiation-convolution-endpoint.md` §2. It
is simply not what G1.6 is for.

## AG5's fingerprint applies ON TOP, not instead

The round-off target is a scalar and can be met for the wrong reason. The
per-DOF fingerprint discriminates:

> **heave barely moves; pitch and surge collapse; the per-DOF change tracks
> `K(0)`.**

That is a *shape*, not a magnitude, and a stub or a mis-synced record cannot
reproduce it by accident. Check the fingerprint before reading the scalar.

## S2 still binds, and binds harder under AF3

The both-directions rule from S2 is not relaxed by moving the target to
round-off — it is the reason AH3's guard is written:

> **A radiation residual of exactly zero is a BROKEN TEST until the fingerprint
> confirms the comparison had something to compare.**

Under the tabulated design a zero would have been obviously wrong. Under AF3 zero
is the *expected* answer, so the failure mode "the gate is comparing a thing to
itself" no longer announces itself. The fingerprint is what replaces that alarm.

**Heave is `0.01×`; pitch and roll are `~44×`.** Four orders of magnitude apart —

### Two honest qualifications

**1. Yaw must be excluded, not reported.** Its `mu` is `1.2e-17` — round-off on a
structurally dead DOF. Including it in the correlation gives `+0.65`; over live
DOF only it is `+0.53`. A statistic computed over a DOF with no signal is the
vacuous-control failure in miniature (eighth guard), and the first version of this
script committed it.

**2. "Tracks `K(0)`" is exact for the damping ratio, approximate for `d|mu|`.**
`endpoint/B_tab` is a property of the kernel and coefficients alone and tracks
`K(0)` exactly. `d|mu|` also depends on **how much that DOF is moving** — roll has
a huge `K(0)` but little motion, so it shows only `−0.7%`. The correct fingerprint
statement is therefore:

> **Check `endpoint/B_tab` for the several-fold shape; check `d|mu|` only for the
> ordering (heave lowest, pitch highest).** Do not expect a high correlation
> between `d|mu|` and `K(0)` — it is `+0.53`, and that is the physics, not a
> defect.
