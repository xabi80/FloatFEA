# G1.6 — expected result, written BEFORE the first run

**Written:** 2026-08-14, before any G1.6 number exists.
**Purpose:** this is the last moment at which a prediction can be made that is
not a rationalisation. Everything below is committed in advance.

---

## Why a per-source prediction and not one threshold

A single tolerance across all sources would be satisfied by the wrong physics.
The three sources reach the record by **different routes**, and their expected
residuals differ by orders of magnitude for reasons that are known now. Writing
one number would hide that, and would let a radiation residual of zero — which
would be a *broken test* — read as an unusually good pass.

## The predictions

### Drag → round-off

**Expected: exactly 0.0, or floating-point round-off.**

Strip and patch loads are **re-evaluated, not reconstructed**. Module 2 already
demonstrated this against `make_morison_state_force`, the closure the integrator
itself calls: max deviation **exactly 0.0**. Nothing about assembling those same
values into a record changes that.

A non-zero drag residual would mean the export lost or reordered something
between evaluation and storage — a plumbing fault, not a physics one.

### Excitation → round-off

**Expected: round-off.**

Excitation is driven by the **incident wave**, which is a pure regular sinusoid.
Fundamental-only reconstruction of a single-frequency drive is exact, so the
coefficient form loses nothing. **The body's nonlinear response does not enter
here** — the incident field is prescribed, not responsive, and that is the whole
reason this source is clean while radiation is not.

### Radiation → NON-ZERO, concentrated at harmonics

**Expected: a non-zero residual with its energy at 2ω and 3ω.**

Radiation is driven by the **body motion**, which is provably non-sinusoidal:
quadratic drag (`u|u|`) and the nonlinear joint constraints both inject harmonic
content. A linear transfer function responds to that content at `B(2ω)`,
`B(3ω)`, and so on.

The two sides of the comparison then differ by construction:

| side | carries harmonics? |
|---|---|
| `mu`, exported directly in the time domain | **yes** — it is the convolution the solver applied |
| panel field, reconstructed at the fundamental | **no** — one frequency by construction |

**The residual *is* the harmonic content.** It is not an error to be driven down;
it is the measurement of how much of the radiation force lives above the
fundamental.

## S2 — the gate must be able to fail in BOTH directions

This is the part most easily lost once a number appears.

> **A radiation residual of zero is a BROKEN TEST, not a perfect
> reconstruction.**

The harmonics cannot have vanished — drag is quadratic and the joints are
nonlinear, so the motion is not a sinusoid and a fundamental-only reconstruction
*must* miss something. Zero means the comparison is not comparing what it claims
to: most likely both sides reconstructed the same way, or the spectral
decomposition is collapsing before it is read.

> **A non-trivial excitation residual is a REAL FINDING, to be chased rather
> than absorbed.**

The prediction above says it should be round-off. If it is not, the leading
candidate is that FloatSim evaluates excitation at the **instantaneous** rather
than the **mean** position — which the surge drift would then modulate through
the changing `kx` phase, since the platform translates ~2.3 spar diameters over
a run. That would be a genuine finding about the load path, and it would connect
two investigations that currently look separate.

Neither outcome is to be met by widening a tolerance.

## S3 — the record must be long enough to trip the panel validity bound

The validity bound is currently exercised **only against a mutated fixture**.
That tests that the rejection fires; it does not test that it fires *on real
data*, which is a different claim.

**The first G1.6 record should therefore be the drift case**, which moves the
platform ~2.3 spar diameters — comfortably past any sensible bound.

> If the bound does **not** trip when the recorded displacement says it should,
> that is a **finding about the bound**, not a clean pass.

A rejection that has never fired outside a test fixture is one you have tested,
not one you have seen work — the same distinction as the argument-versus-
measurement rule in `WORKFLOW.md`.

## Scale caveat, carried

The first G1.6 pass will be on a **model-scale** record; the full-scale re-run is
deferred to F3. F1's closure artifact carries the obligation to **re-run G1.6 on
the first full-scale record**, because a gate whose only passing evidence sits at
a scale that is not the target should be exercised once at the target. The
panel validity bound compounds this: being a displacement, it is scale-specific
and a model-scale bound does not transfer.

# G1.6 FIRST PASS — result, 2026-08-15

Read against the table above before any explanation was constructed.

## Drag — PREDICTION MET

```
body        max |applied|     max |residual|
body0        1.656695e+00      0.000000e+00
body4        1.559920e+00      0.000000e+00
```

**Exactly 0.0.** As predicted: re-evaluated, not reconstructed.

## Radiation — PREDICTION NOT MET, and the table cannot be applied

Predicted: non-zero, energy at 2ω/3ω. Measured, on the worst hydro DOF:

```
                    max|residual|      w            2w            3w
nearest-neighbour   9.852155e-02   4.930437e-02  5.806511e-05  1.110176e-03
interpolated        9.316651e-02   4.522086e-02  3.385346e-04  7.867190e-04
```

The residual is **dominated by the fundamental** — ~130× the 2ω content — which
is the opposite of the prediction.

**My instrumentation is ruled out.** The first pass used a nearest-neighbour
frequency lookup (1.93017 rad/s against a case frequency of 2.00038, a 3.51%
mismatch across a gap where `B` varies ~60%). That is an approximation in the
test and it lands at the fundamental, so it was the first candidate.
Interpolating to the exact frequency moves the fundamental residual by **8%**
— 4.93e-2 to 4.52e-2. Not the explanation.

**But the K2 table must not be applied here, because one of its premises is
false.** "Large residual coherent at the fundamental → phase-convention error"
was written for a comparison against a *steady-state* prediction. The identity
`mu = [A(ω) − A_inf]·ξ̈ + B(ω)·ξ̇` holds **only in steady periodic motion**, and
this record is not in it:

```
post-ramp duration   100.0 s = 31.8 cycles
rotational mode Q    ~134  ->  settling needs ~134 cycles
fraction settled     1 - exp(-pi*n/Q) = 0.53
```

**The record is roughly half-settled.** A transient still ringing at the mode
frequency produces a discrepancy at exactly the fundamental — a third cause the
table did not anticipate, and one that is indistinguishable from a
phase-convention error on this data alone.

## Status: INCONCLUSIVE for radiation, and that is the honest reading

Three candidates remain, in order of prior:

1. **Not at steady state** — quantified above, and sufficient on its own to
   produce a fundamental-coherent residual.
2. A genuine **phase-convention error** in the `mu` export path.
3. Kernel truncation at 60 s interacting with the sub-settled response.

**Distinguishing them needs a longer record**, run to `~Q` cycles rather than
~32. If the residual falls as settling proceeds it is (1); if it plateaus it is
(2) or (3).

Declaring a convention error now would be reading the table past its premise.
Recorded as inconclusive rather than resolved either way.

## Consequence for the K2 table

The table needs a precondition it did not carry: **it applies only to a
steady-state record.** Added here rather than left implicit, because the first
real use of the diagnostic was also the first case that violated its assumption.
