## What was built

Three export modules in HSP, all additive: solve state (with `mu` recovered by
offline replay), strip/patch drag, and panel pressure fields stored as complex
coefficients. Reader and validator in FloatFEA. Machine-readable frame
definitions as the single source, with `docs/conventions.md` generated from them.

**Each reconstruction was verified against the real thing, not argued:**

| module | check | result |
|---|---|---|
| `mu` replay | vs instrumented solver on a throwaway branch | **bit-identical**, 0 of 4806 elements differing — **reproducible by procedure**, recorded below |
| strip/patch | vs `make_morison_state_force` (`HSP:tests/unit/test_flr_strips.py`) | **exactly 0.0** deviation |
| panel fields | vs Capytaine's own resultants (`HSP:tests/unit/test_flr_panels.py`) | **\|ratio\| 1.0000**, all three |

**The replay hook was not needed.** `hsp-coupling.md` reserved one permitted
touch on the solve loop; all three sources turned out to be pure functions of
stored state, so the export is wholly additive and G1.5 holds by construction.
That retired what `PLAN.md` §8 named as F1's largest single risk.

## Carried obligations — to F3

**2. Re-characterise the surge drift at full-scale Reynolds.** The drift balance

and the spar carries almost all the brake at `Cd = 1.2`, a **subcritical** value

## The full-scale deferral, and why F3 rather than F4

Deferred out of F1 by decision, 2026-08-13. F1's gates are scale-agnostic —
G1.1, G1.2, G1.3 and G1.5 trivially, and **G1.6 is a relative conservation
check** holding or failing identically at either scale. F1 tests the plumbing,
not the load values.

The schema reasoning is preserved because it was always about **the schema**: no
scale block, no exponent table, no conversion. Model-scale and full-scale records
pass through the identical reader with identical fields.

**F3 binds, not F4.** G3.1b compares FE mass properties against the FloatSim body
properties that generated the loads, and the FE model is **inherently full
scale** — S355, 8.41 m spar, 180 mm walls. Comparing it against a model-scale
record requires exactly the conversion the decision forbids, not as a convenience
but as the only way to make the comparison at all. F4 is where loads reach the
structure; **F3 is where the scales must already agree.**

## Process finding — a dependency went unread

A locked dependency sat **unstarted and unmentioned for roughly fifteen turns**
while results accumulated on top of it.

What let it persist is the part worth understanding: **every individual status
report was complete and accurate about what it covered.** Nothing was concealed
and nothing was misstated. The dependency simply stopped being re-read after the
milestone opened, while the work in front of it kept generating things worth

**The guard is not "report more"** — more reporting at the same scope would have
changed nothing. It is that **a milestone's stated dependencies are re-read at
every status**, and that dependency lines carry status rather than just names.
Recorded as a practice in `WORKFLOW.md`.

## Version bumps — the discipline working, not instability

The schema went **1.0 → 1.1 → 1.2 before the chain has run once.** That could
read as churn. It is the opposite.

At the v1.0 lock the stated expectation was that the first end-to-end run would
probably break something and that a bump would be the scheme working. What
happened is better than that: **both bumps were caught by review before any
record existed to be wrong.**

- **1.1** — Froude-Krylov and diffraction split. The v1.0 justification for
  merging them was *factually wrong*: Capytaine carries them separately.
- **1.2** — the integrator block. Generalized-alpha forms its RHS at
  alpha-weighted states, so index-aligned export alone is insufficient.

Both would have been discovered at G1.6 as unexplained residuals, and each would
have cost an export change plus a re-run. Locking at 1.0 rather than hedging at
0.9 is what made the bumps legible.

## CORRECTION — G1.1's evidence was wrong when it was written (V2)

The G1.1 row above previously read:

> **PASS** | Writer/reader round-trip in `tests/verification/rung4`

**There was no such test.** `tests/verification/rung4` contained the validator
matrix and nothing else, and `write_solve_state` was referenced nowhere in
FloatFEA's package or tests. The gate was recorded as passing on evidence that did
not exist.

What did exist was adjacent and easy to mistake for it: the G1.2 rejection
matrix's positive control — a well-formed record **hand-built in Python**. It
never touched writer output. So the two halves of the interchange had never met
inside a test, and the one time they did meet, informally, the first end-to-end
pass found `time_convention` missing from the writer.

### Now closed, and it found two defects on contact

**1. The fixture was rejected — correctly.** The first generated record carried
~2 rad rotations while declaring `rotation_validity_bound = 0.1`, and the
validator refused it. The generator was wrong; the validator was right. That is
G1.2's positive control finally doing work against real output.

**2. `FlrValidationError` was a frozen dataclass**, which forbids Python's
`__traceback__` assignment. A rejection propagating through a context manager
therefore arrived as `FrozenInstanceError: cannot assign to field
'__traceback__'` — **the named fault replaced by an unrelated error at exactly
the moment it was needed**. The rejection matrix never saw this because it
catches at the point of raise, where nothing propagates.

A validation error that destroys its own message under the conditions it is
raised in is this project's failure mode applied to the reader itself. Fixed, with
a test asserting the propagation path the matrix does not cover.

### Why the fixture is a committed file, not an import

`CLAUDE.md` says the codebases meet **only at a versioned interchange file**. A
test importing `floatsim` would make that false and make this suite unrunnable
without HSP at a matching state. The boundary object is the fixture, generated by
`artifacts/make_fixture_flr.py` under HSP and committed here. It is a **golden
file** and carries that obligation: regenerating it requires a written explanation
of why the bytes moved.

### `mu` replay — the evidence was deliberately destroyed

The strongest claim in this document is `mu` replay **bit-identical, 0 of 4806
elements differing, vs an instrumented solver**. **No test asserts it**, and none
can: the instrumentation lived on a throwaway branch that was never merged,
precisely because merging it would have violated G1.5's additive-only property.

This is a defensible trade, but the artifact stated it as though it were standing
evidence. What *is* standing: the steady-state radiation identity, the
second-order quadrature guard, and the startup-convention test, all in
`HSP:tests/unit/test_flr_export_mu.py`. Those constrain the replay's arithmetic;
none of them is the byte-comparison against the solver.

**Re-classified (AM4): reproducible by PROCEDURE, not irreproducible.** That is a
different and better category — weaker than a committed test, far stronger than
"no evidence stands". The procedure is recorded below so a future reader re-runs
it in an afternoon rather than inheriting folklore.

#### Procedure — re-establishing the `mu` replay comparison

Instrumentation must NOT be merged: it would put a line in the solve loop and
violate G1.5. Apply it on a throwaway branch, run, record, discard the branch.

**1. Instrument.** In `HSP:floatsim/solver/newmark.py`, the solve loop evaluates
`mu` at one place (currently ~line 448):

```python
        buffer.push(xi_dot_np1)
        mu_np1 = buffer.evaluate()
+       _MU_TRACE.append(mu_np1.copy())        # throwaway instrumentation
```

with `_MU_TRACE: list = []` at module scope, seeded with `np.zeros(n_dof)` for
`mu[0]` to match the startup convention (`mu(0) = 0`, ~line 388 — the
buffer-evaluated value there is the `O(dt)` artifact the integrator skips).

**2. Run** any case that produces a record. The original used the 12-buoy drift
case; `artifacts/make_record.py` reproduces it (12,001 samples x 102 DOF, ~5 min).

**3. Compare** the trace against the offline replay:

```python
from floatsim.io.flr_export import recompute_mu
mu_replay, _ = recompute_mu(setup.kernel, res.xi_dot, from_run_start=True)
mu_solver = np.asarray(_MU_TRACE)
print((mu_replay != mu_solver).sum(), "of", mu_solver.size, "elements differ")
```

**Expected: `0 of N`, exactly.** Not "close" — `recompute_mu` replays the same
`RadiationConvolution.evaluate()` the solver calls, so any non-zero count means
the replay has diverged from the solve path and the whole `mu` channel is
suspect. The original figure was `0 of 4806` on a shorter record.

**4. Discard the branch.** `HSP:tests/unit/test_export_is_additive.py` fails if
instrumentation survives into `floatsim/`.

*Caveat carried:* this pins the replay to the solver **as it is at the time of
running**. It was last performed before the trapezoid fix (`9fb5b33`), which
changed `evaluate()` — both sides changed together, so the result should still be
`0`, but that has not been re-run.

## AN2 — G1.6 moved into CI

The identity is **size-independent** — both sides are the same surface integral —
so it does not need the 12-buoy platform:

```
F_j = -sum_p  p_p (n_p . d_j,p) A_p     ==     w^2 A_jk + i w B_jk
```

The committed fixture is **two spheres, 64 wetted panels, 12 DOF, one frequency**,
13.6 kB, generated in seconds by `artifacts/make_panel_fixture.py`. Residual at
generation `1.518e-16`; the full-platform run measures `1.308e-15`.

`floatfea/tolerances.py` gains `PANEL_RECONSTRUCTION_RESIDUAL = 1e-12` — three
orders above the measured residual, seven orders *below* the smallest deliberate
defect (one panel of 10,560 perturbed 1%, giving `1.082e-05`). That gap is what
makes the ceiling defensible rather than fitted.

The test carries its own failure mode: perturbing one panel must move the residual
by ≥3 orders, or the gate is declared to be measuring nothing.

**Two limitations found while writing it, both recorded in the test:**

The fixture sets `rotation_center` to each body's own origin so Capytaine's
rotational DOF share the record's moment reference. **On the real platform they
differ**, and that transformation is *not* exercised here — it belongs to F4.

A first version tried to assert non-circularity numerically, by requiring
`Re(R)/ω²` to differ from `A`. That is asserting the identity is **false**, since
`Re(T) = ω²A` is exactly what the identity says. The circularity worth guarding is
one of **provenance** — whether `A`/`B` were read from the solver or derived from
the panel field — and provenance is invisible in a pass/fail (tenth guard). It is
guaranteed by the generator, not by the test, and the test now says so instead of
pretending otherwise.
