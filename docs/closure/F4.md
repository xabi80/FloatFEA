| gate | what it asserts today |
|---|---|
| **G4.4** | mapped total force and moment about a common point, per body and per source, against the record's own resultant |
| **DQ4(i)** | per node, `M a` against the closed-form consistent-load construction, and the resultant moment about `G` |
| **DQ4(ii)** | the element nodal vector under uniform translational acceleration, against `μL/2` and `±μL²/12`, **signed** |
| **DQ5 / G4.2** | `a = g`, `α = 0` and member forces under a uniform field, through DY0's split and never through `M` |
| **G4.1 static** | `abs(Σ reactions − weight) / weight`, per body |
| **G4.1 dynamic** | **two** dimensionless residuals, each gated and never combined, per FE body and per case over the DQ6 window, recomputed **from committed inputs** with no FloatSim and no HSP worktree |
| **EB6 / DQ9** | each buoy and hub label's node coordinates, against an independent read |
| **EK0(a)** | the external hydrodynamic force on the five FE bodies is identically zero |
| **EK0(d)/(e)** | the static supports carry nothing, the platform's four hub reactions are equal by symmetry, and `res.lam` is equal and opposite on each joint's two bodies |

**The quantity G4.1 dynamic gates, in full:**

```
force  :  max_t |Σ_j F_j − M·a|                          /  max_t Σ_j |F_j|
moment :  max_t |Σ_j (r_j×F_j + M_j) − J·α − …|          /  max_t Σ_j (|r_j×F_j| + |M_j|)
```

Denominators are **sums of magnitudes**, so cancellation in a quiet window cannot shrink
them. Moments are about each body's `reference_point` (`docs/conventions.md:165`), not `G`
as EV1's wording says; `cog_offset_body=None` makes the two coincide in-solve.

## 4. What carries into F6's ledger — BLOCKING, by name

**R730 — the MID station was not an internal force, and it reached a sent deliverable.**
`scripts/measure/member_forces_table.py` averaged the two raw element end forces.
`member_forces` returns the forces the element *exerts on its nodes*, so those are an action
and a reaction; averaging them computes a **load**. The arithmetic is exact about it:
conservation gives `Vz_a + Vz_b = weight`, so the MID `Vz` was **half the member's weight**.

```
platform:hub2_arm, static   published            corrected
  MID Vz                    +2299218.75          -3832031.25
  MID My                     6.7060547e+07        1.2454102e+08
  MID sigma                  94.4 MPa             175.3 MPa      (46% LOW)
```

**Repaired, and then the repair was wrong too (R734).** All three stations are now one
convention, so `TIP` is `-end_b`, and the shear profile is monotone where it had a
spurious zero crossing. But the first repair **calibrated** the parabolic sign against
one measured cell instead of deriving it, and gave BOTH bending planes the same sign.
They carry opposite signs: `e_x x F = (0, -F_z, +F_y)`, so `M_y'' = +w_z` and
`M_z'' = -w_y`, giving sags of `-w_z L^2/8` and `+w_y L^2/8`.

**The calibration could not see it.** `w_local[1]` is **exactly `0.0` on all sixteen
members on the static basis**, so the `Mz` term is unobservable there -- and both of my
published cells, both static controls my own closing condition asked for, and both of the
reviewer's independent figures sat at `w_y = 0`. **A control that can only be taken where
the quantity is zero is not a control.** 281 of 384 MID dynamic/total rows moved, worst
`26.3%` on a dynamic row, **low in all twelve of the worst twelve**.

Now derived rather than calibrated, with a **sign control that runs where `w_y != 0` and
needs no threshold**: it refuses if either sag disagrees with the sign its own load
component implies, and it catches the shipped defect on the first member.

**It carries into F6 as blocking anyway**, because the closed-form MID remains unverified
against a refined mesh -- a measured `4.0%` approximation on the bending term.

**F6 step 1 must not form a code check on the MID column until R730's conditions are fully
discharged. ROOT and TIP are verified exact and a check on those two may start at once.**

**R731 — `hsp_tag` was recorded and compared with nothing**, and it is the gate's stated
warrant for leaving FloatSim's own files off the staleness list. Repaired here — compared
against `floatfea/hsp_pin.py:HSP_TAG`, one string, no worktree needed — and it carries as
blocking until F6 confirms the warrant holds for every input the tag is claimed to cover.

**R732 -- the 1.55% acceleration agreement is the generalized-alpha mass weighting**, not
a mass difference as I had attributed it. FE rigid mass / npz `body_mass` is
`1.00000000` on all five bodies. Re-measured over **1203 body-step samples**, every fifth
step of all six cases: the alpha_m-weighted reference is `0.0000%` in all six cases
against `1.4453 / 1.1963 / 1.0767 / 1.0093 / 0.9370 / 0.8133%` unweighted -- ratios
`3.67e+12x` to `2.06e+13x`. **This is R711's finding one layer out**, and section 5's
claim that F6 has to build the gate and not the physics is earned rather than hopeful.

## 6. The limitations every F4 result carries

* **The stand-in tube** is the stiffness equivalent of a triangulated truss of undecided
  depth (`F1.md:390`). Stresses are indicative, not a check on a real section.
* **The equivalent density of the hub arms is above steel** — `11433.5 kg/m³` against
  `7850` — so the mass does not fit inside F1's section at `f = 0.75` (DZ5 / ER1(e)).
* **All six cases are heading 0°. The heading dependence is UNTESTED** (EX3 / R724), and it
  matters most exactly where the governing load is: the transverse arms' `Mz = 4.94e+08 N·m`
  is entirely dynamic, because they sit on the `y` axis and the wave runs along `x`.
* **The vacuous moment-channel set** is `hub1/3` and `hub3/11` in 6 of 6 cases. The earlier
  published cause — that two hubs' platform joints sit at their reference points and two do
  not — is **withdrawn**: all four carry `attach_a_body: [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]`. What is known is
  that those are the two hubs on the wave axis.
* **The envelope is per component**, so a stress formed from it is an upper bound. The
  per-instant value is the physical one and both are reported.

**EZ2 corrected the envelope basis, and the governing number dropped by a third.** For
`H = 24.2 m` the associated-period band is `sqrt(6.5 H) .. sqrt(11 H)` = `12.542 .. 16.316 s`.
`T = 10 s` has `H/lambda = 0.1550`, which **exceeds the deep-water breaking limit**
`1/7 = 0.1429`, and `T = 20 s` is too long for the height. Neither may set a design
envelope.

| basis | governing member-station | case | sigma at the instant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **as first published** (all six cases) | `platform:hub2_arm` ROOT | `T = 10 s` | **742.6 MPa** |
| **EZ2, in-range cases only** | `platform:hub4_arm` ROOT | `T = 12.5 s` | **455.7 MPa** |

The two out-of-range cases are reported in their own block as sensitivity only, because
`T = 10 s` is still the largest response in the sweep and a reader who saw only the
governing block would not know that.

**And `T = 12.5 s` sits `0.042 s` BELOW the band it is included under.** EZ2(a) names it as
governing and that is what ships, but the directive's own criterion and its own case list
disagree by that much -- and it is the lowest-period governing case, so it carries **8 of
the governing top ten**. The envelope is therefore sensitive to a boundary the basis does
not quite satisfy, which is a question for the load basis and not for the FE.

**The headline finding is still about the section, not the platform.** Static self-weight
alone puts the platform root at `269.7 MPa` at the basis `f = 0.75` against a `213.0 MPa`
allowable, and over it at every rung of the `f` ladder; with the governing wave,
`455.7 MPa`. That is the density-above-steel finding seen from the other side.
